Catalog v4: science questions by region

Cascadia OBS ensemble catalog v4 (Ian's final catalog on the ver6 picks), 2010–2016 · literature, open questions, a fault-model plan · 21 November 2026

For each subregion of the margin, this note lists five peer-reviewed papers to interpret what v4 shows, and the open questions a 2010–2016 catalog can realistically answer. It ends with what a seismicity-based 3D fault model would add, and how to put uncertainties on its surfaces. The v4 magnitudes (our Route A ML) were computed today and are summarized first. A 3D atlas of the catalog with faults, slab and coupling sits next to this file.

Read before interpreting anything below
  • Depth datum. v4 depths are relative to an unconfirmed datum, sea level or seafloor. Offshore, water depth is 1.5–4 km, about the thickness of the oceanic crust. Every crust/mantle or interface/slab statement waits on Ian's answer.
  • Absolute locations. v4 is not relatively relocated. Fault-scale structure (planes, ring faults, cluster widths) needs cross-correlation double-difference relocation first.
  • Station coverage changes by year. Rates follow deployments: X9 (Sep 2012–Oct 2013, Blanco), Z5 (Sep 2013–Oct 2015, 40–44.7°N), 7D (Jul 2011–Oct 2015), OO at Axial (from late 2014). New finding: south of 42°N, the NC and BK networks contribute almost no v4 picks in 2010–2011 (NC: 456 picks in 2010, against 51,092 in 2014) and none in 2016. Events there in 2010–2011 rest on PB borehole and TA stations. Check whether NC data are missing from the pick tables for those years before reading any Mendocino or Gorda rate change.
  • Magnitudes are new (below). Use a completeness magnitude per region and per deployment period before any b-value or productivity comparison.
  • Sources. Five research passes found the papers, and every DOI was checked against its Crossref record. A few content notes rest on the abstract or on the reviewer's knowledge of the paper; they are marked. Three citations need a check on the publisher page (listed at the end).

Endeavour, Endeavour–Explorer junction, Sovanco, Nootka

v4 has 2,439 events here (1,019 QC), 1.5 times v3, with 39% of the picks new (7D, CN, NV). It records the junction, the Sovanco fracture zone, the Nootka area and a cluster near 47.6°N. On the Endeavour segment near the 48°N vent fields there are 283 events (39 QC), against 13 in v3. Shared events moved 8.7 km shallower between v3 and v4, and the median QC depth of 12.4 km is deep for a ridge. Ian should also explain the 906 v3 events that v4 drops.

Questions

How does seismicity divide between Sovanco, the junction and Nootka?

AnalysisMap QC events against the fault traces of Rohr et al. (2018, 10.1029/2018GC007851) and Hutchinson et al. (2019, 2023); compare matched mechanisms with Braunmiller & Nábělek (2002). LimitsThe area sits at the edge of both networks; epicentral errors of several km blur fault-scale structure.

Are the deep hypocentres real?

AnalysisAfter the datum is fixed, compare depths on-axis (expected < 3 km) with Nootka and Sovanco, and test sensitivity to the velocity model and to the presence of 7D stations. LimitsStations lie mostly to the east, so depth trades off with origin time. Deep on-axis events after these checks would point to a location problem, not ridge physics.

What was the Endeavour segment doing in 2011–2016?

AnalysisCompare our on-segment rate with Krauss et al. (2025). Limits39 QC events; small on-axis events are under-detected without local stations, so our count is an upper bound on quiet.

What is the 47.6°N cluster?

AnalysisTiming (single swarm or persistent), waveform similarity, dependence on 7D stations. LimitsCould be an association artifact; no independent catalog unless ComCat or the Canadian catalog has it.

Axial Seamount

New in v4: 26,697 events (19,186 QC), all from the OOI cabled array (OO), on the seafloor since late 2014 and first picked in ver5. The rate climbs from 672 events per month in November 2014 to 8,147 in March 2015, before the 24 April 2015 eruption, drops to 566 in May, and runs near 100 a month into 2016. Events form two lobes along the caldera rim and the south rift, between about −2 and 2 km, with a second cluster at 4–5 km. The caldera floor is about 1.5 km below sea level, so the datum matters most here.

Questions

Does an independent ML catalog reproduce the pre-eruption acceleration, and does it find more or fewer events than the double-difference and real-time ML catalogs?

AnalysisMatch events in time with Waldhauser et al. (2020) and Wang et al. (2024); compare daily rates, the shape of the acceleration, and the timing of the drop. LimitsNo baseline before late 2014; the catalog ends in 2016.

Does b-value change across the eruption, and does rate follow inflation?

AnalysisMoving-window b and Mc against the Nooner & Chadwick uplift curve. LimitsML calibration for very small, close-recorded events; Mc rises when events overlap at peak rate. Without careful magnitudes this is a consistency check on Tan et al. (2019), not a new result.

Is the 4–5 km seismicity real?

AnalysisFix the datum, relocate a subset in the Baillard et al. 3-D model, test network dependence and timing. LimitsA 1-D model biases depths under the low-velocity reservoir.

Offshore Washington forearc

2,755 QC events (1,864 in v3), 300–650 a year in 2010–2015, median depth 17.8 km (10–90%: 0.9–30.5 km). Coverage depends on 7D (2011–2015) and coastal stations. The earlier Cascadia Initiative catalog found this part of the margin sparse (Stone et al. 2018), so ours is a large increase that needs checking for false associations.

Questions

Is the shallow Washington megathrust aseismic, or are events missed there?

AnalysisMap detection completeness against distance to the deformation front (synthetic events or station-subset bootstrap), then count events within ±5 km of the interface (Slab2 or CASIE21) by distance to the trench, against Morton et al. (2023) and Stone et al. (2018). LimitsOffshore depth errors and the datum; a quiet zone gives only an upper bound on rate, and cannot separate locked from creeping without seafloor geodesy.

Does offshore seismicity follow coupling or structure?

AnalysisAssign each event its coupling under all six models we hold (data/coupling/) and test for density changes at 45.8–46°N and at the Watt & Brothers boundaries, with permutation tests. LimitsThe coupling models disagree offshore; results must hold across them.

Does the rate change around ETS episodes?

AnalysisDeclustered near-interface rates around PNSN tremor episodes. Limits7D redeployments change detection year to year; needs a fixed station set or M above completeness; low power with a few hundred events a year.

Puget Lowland

9,204 QC events (4,704 in v3), 1,200–1,600 a year through 2016, median depth 15.6 km: mostly crustal, with fewer intraslab events at 40–60 km. Part of the doubling comes from the 122–123°W strip added in ver6. The 10% depth of 1.1 km suggests quarry blasts to remove.

Questions

Is crustal seismicity concentrated on the Seattle, Tacoma and SWIF zones, or diffuse?

AnalysisRelocate (3-D model or double-difference), measure distance to the CRESCENT CFM fault surfaces at depth against randomized catalogs, check mechanisms against N–S compression. LimitsBlasts, basin bias, and the new station strip. No published study images SWIF or Tacoma at depth from seismicity, so a positive result would be new.

Does crustal or intraslab rate change with ETS?

AnalysisStack rates around the five or so 2010–2016 episodes by depth class. LimitsSmall sample; the effect appears at M > 2.5, where we have few events.

Do deep events split at the slab Moho?

AnalysisCross-sections against McCrory et al. (2012) and the Preston et al. reflector. LimitsNeeds depth errors well below the ~7 km slab crust, which a land network at 40–60 km may not give.

Offshore Oregon forearc

1,063 QC events (808 in v3), 60–270 a year, median depth 10.0 km (10–90%: 0.0–30.0 km). It includes the persistent clusters near 44.5°N and events seaward of the deformation front. The 10th percentile at 0.0 km suggests events at a fixed or default depth; flag them first.

Questions

Are the Oregon clusters plate-boundary events on fixed structure, or upper-plate faulting?

AnalysisRelative relocation of the 44.5°N and Heceta/Stonewall clusters; depth against CASIE21; polarity mechanisms to separate low-angle thrusts. LimitsDatum (water 0.1–3 km); azimuthal coverage only part of the time.

Does event density follow locking, when the locking models disagree?

AnalysisDensity against Schmalzle (creep) and Pearson & Schmidt (locked), and against the Carbotte domains. If density follows structure under both, that points to structural control. LimitsFew events, dominated by clusters; completeness changes as OBS sites move.

What do events seaward of the deformation front say about the incoming plate?

AnalysisAgainst bending faults and propagator wakes (Han et al. 2018; Carbotte et al. 2024) and the 440 seaward events of Stone et al. LimitsOutside the array: locations yes, mechanisms no.

Blanco transform (with northern Gorda Ridge)

42,806 events (21,082 QC), 18 times v3. The gain comes from X9 (Sep 2012–Oct 2013) and Z5 (Sep 2013–Oct 2015), first picked in ver5. QC events per year: 3,660 (2012), 10,231 (2013), 4,691 (2014), 2,319 (2015). The seismicity outlines the transform segment by segment. Median depth 8.7 km, with vertical columns where depth is poorly constrained. Four recent papers all use the one-year X9 array; none uses 2013–2015. Z5 covers the Blanco in 2013–2015, so our multi-year view of the transform is likely new.

Questions

How does seismic coupling vary along strike, and does the pattern hold over 2012–2015?

AnalysisCounts and, with magnitudes, summed moment per segment per year against the Braunmiller & Nábělek slip rates; test whether the step-over contrast and the extensional gaps persist beyond the X9 year. LimitsCompleteness changes between X9 and Z5; four years is short next to M6 recurrence (10–20 yr), so rates and b-values are more robust than moment sums.

Are the deep events mantle swarms, or poor depth control?

AnalysisRestrict to events with an OBS within about one focal depth; compare with the 600 °C isotherm and with Journeau and Lange for the overlap year; relocate in the Lange 3-D model. LimitsThe datum shifts depth by about a crustal thickness here.

Do swarms migrate, and do they precede larger events?

AnalysisSwarm detection, migration fits, activity before the M5+ events of 2012–2015. LimitsFew M5+ events; repeaters need waveform correlation.

Gorda plate

28,610 QC events (21,997 in v3) in every year 2010–2015, median depth 21.5 km. It includes the January 2010 M6.5 and March 2014 M6.8 offshore events. The 2010–2011 events rest on PB and TA stations, not NC (see the box at the top).

Questions

Do small-event lineations in 2010–2015 mark inherited weak zones, including the future 2022 Ferndale fault?

AnalysisPlanar clusters against the 1975/2022, 1980 and 2005 ruptures. A spatial test that does not need magnitudes. LimitsNeeds relative relocation; six years cannot show recurrence.

How is deformation shared between plate-interior faults, the slab and the megathrust?

AnalysisClassify events against Slab2 and the Guo et al. interface; compare lineation strikes with Chaytor et al. and with our matched tensors. LimitsDatum; offshore west of ~125.5°W only 7D and distant land stations.

Aftershocks of the 2010 M6.5 and 2014 M6.8 events: productivity, decay, triggering.

AnalysisETAS or Omori fits above completeness, against the Rollins & Stein stress pattern. LimitsCoverage in early 2010 is land-only and without NC; completeness right after each mainshock is poor.

Mendocino triple junction

25,460 QC events (19,253 in v3), median depth 21.6 km. Counts per year jump from 2,831 (2013) to 6,549 (2014). Part of that is the March 2014 M6.8 sequence, and part is coverage: NC picks double from 2013 to 2014, and Z5 arrives. 2016 has 13 QC events because NC and BK give no picks that year.

Questions

What share of seismicity is on the Gorda–North America interface, against Gorda crust and mantle, North American crust and the Mendocino fault?

AnalysisProject hypocentres onto McCrory et al. (2012) or Gong & McGuire (2021); count by depth relative to the slab, by year; compare shared events with Waldhauser. LimitsA few km of datum error moves events across the interface; without per-event uncertainty the share is a range.

Did aseismic slip on the Mendocino fault change after the 2014 M6.8 and 2015 M5.7?

AnalysisFind repeater candidates in tight clusters, confirm by waveform correlation, compare recurrence with Materna et al. LimitsNeeds waveforms (we have them); moment–slip scaling needs magnitudes.

Is seismicity modulated seasonally?

AnalysisMonthly rates above completeness against wave height. LimitsSix years, coverage gaps (2010–2011, 2016), and noise-dependent ML detection; test above a conservative magnitude.

A 3D fault model from v4

A margin-wide fault model from this catalog alone is out of reach. A set of seismicity-derived fault surfaces, used to update and test the existing models, is not. The case rests on one fact from the review of existing models: none of the 3D fault geometries in Cascadia comes from hypocentres.

Methods to build surfaces from hypocentres

  • Clustering of planes: Ouillon et al. (2008) 10.1029/2007JB005032; with location uncertainty, Wang et al. (2013) 10.1002/2013JB010164; agglomerative, Kamer et al. (2020) 10.5194/nhess-20-3611-2020.
  • Implicit surfaces from traces, nodal planes and hypocentres: Riesner et al. (2017) 10.1785/0220160192; applied to Ridgecrest for the SCEC CFM, Plesch et al. (2020) 10.1785/0120200053.
  • Density clustering plus PCA: Truttmann et al. (2023) 10.1029/2023JB026352; Piegari et al. (2024) 10.1029/2023EA003267.
  • SURF (USGS Python): clustering, merging by orientation, non-planar meshes. Alongi et al. (2025) 10.1785/0220250126. The most usable tool; one author also works on NSHM and the CRESCENT CFM.
  • ML catalog example: Ross et al. (2020), Cahuilla swarm, 10.1126/science.abb0779.

Where to start

Mendocino and the Gorda plate first. It is the most active area, recorded from land and sea, and the existing model is weakest there (a vertical Mendocino plane, no Gorda faults). Independent catalogs exist to check against (Yoon & Shelly 2024; Hsu et al. 2026). Blanco second: absent from every fault model, with four independent X9 catalogs for the overlap year. Axial and Endeavour already have denser cabled-array catalogs; Puget seismicity is diffuse. Either pilot needs three things first: the depth datum, double-difference relocation, and per-event location uncertainty.

Uncertainty on fault surfaces

Yes, people care, and the hazard community most of all. Dip is one of the parameters hazard results are most sensitive to, and the CFM assigns default dips with no uncertainty. A surface without an uncertainty cannot enter a logic tree. The plan is to give each surface a mean geometry plus an ensemble, and to keep apart the sources of error that behave differently.

SourceHow to quantifyWhat it gives
Location error (random)Monte Carlo: perturb every hypocentre within its uncertainty (covariance from double-difference bootstrap, or v4's spatial_sigma until then), rerun clustering and fitting N times (Wang et al. 2013 do this inside the fit).Per-vertex spread normal to the surface; strike and dip distributions.
SamplingBootstrap the events of each cluster.How much a surface depends on a few events; unstable surfaces get flagged.
Method choicesVary clustering parameters (minimum cluster size, merge angle) and surface smoothness over a sensible range.Whether a fault is robust or an artefact of the settings.
Velocity model and datum (systematic)Relocate with 2–3 models (1-D, CRESCENT or regional 3-D, e.g. Guo et al. 2021, Baillard et al. 2019) and both datums.A systematic shift, reported separately: it moves all surfaces together and does not average out.
Width vs errorCompare the spread of events normal to each surface with the expected location error.If the spread is much larger than the error, the zone is truly wide (distributed deformation); if similar, the width is unresolved.
Independent checksAngle between the surface and focal-mechanism nodal planes (our matched tensors, later the polarity mechanisms); dips from seismic lines (CASIE21, CFM lines).An external test of each dip.

Each surface would ship as a mean mesh with a per-vertex uncertainty (km, normal to the surface), strike and dip with 90% intervals, the number of events, a mask of where events constrain it versus where it is extrapolated, and the ensemble of meshes. The atlas can show the uncertainty as colour on the surface or as an envelope.

The 3D atlas

data/catalog_v4/atlas/cascadia_v4_atlas.html is a single 8.6 MB file in the style of the Cascadia Offshore Sensor Atlas (RCA atlas). It is built by workflow/06_analysis/build_v4_atlas.py. It shows:

  • v4 events with our magnitudes, coloured by depth, time or share of picks added after ver3;
  • GMRT and GMT bathymetry, which can be made see-through or cut away to look from below;
  • Slab2, coloured by depth or by any of the six coupling models;
  • CASIE21, the CRESCENT CFM meshes and traces, plate boundaries, stations, and ComCat moment tensors as beachballs.

Filters cover time (drag on the histogram, or play a moving window), magnitude, Ian's location sigma, depth and QC. The cross-section tool draws events, terrain, Slab2 and the CFM and CASIE21 intersections, with no vertical exaggeration by default. It needs a browser with WebGL and an internet connection for three.js. Open it from a copy on your laptop, or serve it with python -m http.server and forward the port.
