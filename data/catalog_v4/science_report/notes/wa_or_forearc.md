# Offshore Washington and Oregon forearc (agent report, 2026-11-21; DOIs checked on Crossref)
Agent note: content notes for Tréhu 2015, Morton 2018, Schmalzle 2014, Lindsey 2021 come from title + model knowledge (no Crossref abstract) — check text before citing.

## Offshore Washington (46.2–49.2°N)
Papers:
1. Stone, Vidale, Han & Roland (2018). Catalog of offshore seismicity in Cascadia: Insights into the regional distribution of microseismicity and its relation to subduction processes. JGR Solid Earth 123(1), 641–652. 10.1002/2017JB014966 — STA/LTA catalog from the same CI OBS: 271 forearc events M0.4–4.0 + 440 seaward of the deformation front; sparse 49–46°N, more 46–45°N near-interface. Our 2,755 QC events is a large increase: show where the new events are and rule out false associations.
2. Morton, Bilek & Rowe (2023). Cascadia subduction zone fault heterogeneities from newly detected small magnitude earthquakes. JGR Solid Earth 128(6), e2023JB026607. 10.1029/2023JB026607 — Subspace detection on CI: 5,282 events (4,096 new); downdip edge of interplate seismicity matches Schmalzle 20% locking contour; no updip edge found; possible asperities.
3. Li, Wang, Wang, Jiang & Dosso (2018). Geodetically inferred locking state of the Cascadia megathrust based on a viscoelastic Earth model. JGR Solid Earth 123(9), 8056–8072. 10.1029/2018JB015620 — Narrow shallow locked zone; land geodesy cannot resolve shallowest far-offshore creep.
4. Han, Carbotte, Canales, Nedimović & Carton (2018). Along-trench structural variations of the subducting Juan de Fuca plate from multichannel seismic reflection imaging. JGR Solid Earth 123(4), 3122–3146. 10.1002/2017JB015059 — Bending faults/hydration change near 45.8°N (less faulted north); seamount and propagator wakes under sediment.
5. Watt & Brothers (2020). Systematic characterization of morphotectonic variability along the Cascadia convergent margin: Implications for shallow megathrust behavior and tsunami hazards. Geosphere 17(1), 95–117. 10.1130/GES02178.1 — Margin segments by wedge morphology and vergence; WA wide, concave, landward vergence.
Questions:
1. Is the shallow WA megathrust aseismic or under-detected? Mc/detection vs distance to deformation front (synthetics or station-subset bootstrap), counts within ±5 km of Slab2/Carbotte 2024 interface by distance-to-trench bin, vs Morton 2023 / Stone 2018. Limits: offshore depth errors, datum, Mc pending; quiet zone gives only an upper bound; locked vs creeping needs seafloor geodesy.
2. Does offshore seismicity follow coupling or structure? Coupling value per event under several models (Schmalzle 2014, Li 2018, Lindsey 2021, Pearson & Schmidt 2026); depth class; density change at 45.8–46°N and Watt & Brothers boundaries; permutation tests. Limits: models disagree offshore; depth blurs classes.
3. Rate steady or changing around ETS? Near-interface subset, declustered, vs PNSN tremor. Limits: 7D redeployment changes detection; needs fixed stations or M>Mc; low power.

## Offshore Oregon (42.8–46.4°N)
Papers:
1. Tréhu, Braunmiller & Davis (2015). Seismicity of the central Cascadia continental margin near 44.5°N: A decadal view. SRL 86(3), 819–829. 10.1785/0220140207 — Persistent clusters near 44.5°N; check our clusters, rates, depths.
2. Morton, Bilek & Rowe (2018). Newly detected earthquakes in the Cascadia subduction zone linked to seamount subduction and deformed upper plate. Geology 46(11), 943–946. 10.1130/G45354.1 — Structure-control hypothesis off central Oregon.
3. Schmalzle, McCaffrey & Creager (2014). Central Cascadia subduction zone creep. G3 15(4), 1515–1532. 10.1002/2013GC005172 — Partial creep off central Oregon; reason to expect more interface microseismicity than WA.
4. Pearson & Schmidt (2026). The distribution of interseismic locking on the Cascadia subduction zone using Bayesian methods. Seismica 2(4). 10.26443/seismica.v2i4.1701 — Bayesian locking with seafloor GNSS; strongest locking near deformation front and central Oregon (disagrees with Schmalzle). CAVEAT: Crossref says vol 2 issue 4 published 2026-09-02 but Seismica vol 2 is normally 2023 — check on the Seismica page.
5. Carbotte, Boston, Han, Shuck, Beeson, Canales, Tobin, Miller, Nedimović, Tréhu et al. (2024). Subducting plate structure and megathrust morphology from deep seismic imaging linked to earthquake rupture segmentation at Cascadia. Science Advances 10(23), eadl3198. 10.1126/sciadv.adl3198 — CASIE21 imaged interface (better reference than Slab2), domains bounded by lower-plate strike-slip faults. Also relevant for WA.
Questions:
1. Are the Oregon clusters plate-boundary events on fixed structure or upper-plate faulting? Differential-time relocation of 44.5°N and Heceta/Stonewall clusters; depth vs Carbotte interface; polarity mechanisms. Limits: q10 depth 0.0 km suggests fixed/default depths to flag; datum (water 0.1–3 km); azimuthal coverage.
2. Does along-strike density follow locking when models disagree? Density vs Schmalzle (creep) and Pearson & Schmidt (locked) and Carbotte domains. Limits: 60–270 QC/yr dominated by clusters; completeness changes with OBS moves.
3. What do events seaward of the deformation front say about incoming-plate stress? vs Han 2018, Carbotte 2024, Stone 2018 seaward events. Limits: outside array, poor depth.
4. (needs other data) Locked-quiet vs creeping-quiet needs seafloor geodesy or repeaters; catalog can test for repeaters via waveform similarity.
