Fault surfaces v0: literature matches

145 rectangular faults (155 found, 10 removed after this check) fitted to catalog v4 seismicity, each compared with published faults, ridge and transform segments and seismic zones. Searches done 2026-11-22, one per region; every DOI was checked on Crossref or DataCite unless marked in the notes. Strikes and map positions were compared; v4 depths are biased shallow and its dips are weak.

Read first
  • Most surfaces are known structures. 99 of the 145 kept surfaces match a published fault, ridge segment, transform strand or seismic zone; 26 of them with high or medium-high confidence. Examples: Blanco Ridge (BL003, BL013, strike within 2°), the Cascadia Depression normal faults (BL005, BL008, BL021), the Escanaba Trough and the Jackson and Central Gorda Ridge segments (GO002, GO008, GO003), the faults of the 2010 Mw 6.5 and 2014 Mw 6.8 Gorda earthquakes (MT014, MT024; MT008, MT012), the 2005 Mw 7.2 and Mw 6.6 fault zones (GO015, GO013), the Garberville swarm (MT009, the buried eastward Mendocino transform of Shelly et al. 2025, 2026), the St. Helens seismic zone (ON001, ON003), the Twin Lakes segment of the Mount Hood fault zone (ON015), the Portland Hills fault at depth (ON013), the Leech River fault zone (PU011), and the fault of the 2014 Mw 6.4 Nootka sequence (EN003).
  • Map positions are good; depths are not. On the Gorda Ridge, v4 epicentres sit on the bathymetric axial valley, while ComCat puts the same events 10–20 km east. But nine Blanco surfaces reach below 25 km, where published Blanco seismicity stops at 10–14 km, and off Oregon ComCat puts the OR001 cluster at 26–33 km against 6 km in v4. The relocation (Track A) is needed before dips and depths are interpreted.
  • Several fits join structures that the literature separates. The long western Blanco surfaces (BL001, BL002, BL006, BL009) cross right-stepping strands; the large Mendocino surfaces (MT001–MT007) are the two layers of the double seismic zone in the Gorda slab, not single faults; the broad low-dip Puget planes (PU001, PU002, PU005, PU013) are diffuse basement seismicity. 17 surfaces duplicate a neighbour.
  • Candidate new structures (35 flagged). The strongest: a WNW right-lateral source in the Gorda plate near 41.85°N 126.1°W with repeated M4–5.7 events (GO010, GO011, GO017); the 2012 Brookings swarm (GO020); a plane on the Cleft segment of the Juan de Fuca Ridge (BL025); a north-dipping E–W structure east of 122.1°W that could continue the Tacoma fault (PU015, PU018); an E–W vertical alignment north of the Mendocino transform (MT020); a NNW dextral strand west of the Eaton Roughs fault (MT017); and a steep N-striking zone at Salmon Inlet, BC, active in 2015, about 4 km from the 2025 Mww 4.8 epicentre (ON022).
  • Removed after this check. Six surfaces that were mostly quarry blasts (ON012, ON020, ON023, ON029, ON037, ON040) and four likely artefacts (BL022, BL024, BL026, PU010) are no longer in the product; they are listed below and in fault_surfaces_v0_removed.csv. The 2010–2016 catalog has no intraslab surface under Puget Sound and no surface on the Leech River trace itself, Klamath Falls, Daisy Bank or Wecoma.

(c) Possible new or unmapped structures

  1. BL025 (Cleft segment, 44.6–44.84°N): a coherent, stable, ridge-parallel seismic plane about 20 km N of the RTI. The OBS studies (2012–13) describe the JdFR as nearly aseismic. If BL025 does not come from one transient episode (check event times), it is a seismogenic structure that has not been reported on the southern Cleft segment.
  2. BL010 (EBD–SUR oblique cross fault, strike 143°, dip about 66°): no named fault at this spot. It may be a pull-apart cross-basin normal or transtensional fault. Worth checking against SeaBeam lineaments (Dauteuil 1995).
  3. BL014 (structure inside the GD / S of the BR): possibly the trough fault BN08 proposed from a few strike-slip events. If confirmed, it would be a third parallel strand between the GD N wall (BL016) and the GT (BL017).
  4. BL011 (BR–CAS junction, 54° dip, N-shifted): this matches the seismicity–bathymetry misalignment Lange reported and the NE fault bend Journeau reported, but no one has mapped a fault plane there. A moderately dipping transtensional link would be new geometry, provided the dip survives relocation.
  5. Not recovered: no surface lies on the Parks Plateau Fault or the north-of-BR lineament near 43.5°N 127.25°W (BN08 §45), and none on Lange's off-axis cluster at 44.4°N 129.68°W. Their absence is consistent with the literature, which describes these as nearly aseismic or as very short bursts.

Caveats for interpretation

  • Strikes of 120–124° on the long western surfaces (BL001, BL002, BL006, BL009) are about 10–13° clockwise of plate motion. That is the signature of an envelope across right-stepping en-echelon strands, not the strike of an individual fault. The OBS-resolved strands are parallel to plate motion.
  • Surfaces whose bottom lies below about 15 km (BL017, BL022–BL024, BL026; also BL005/007/008/012 at 25–30 km) go deeper than any published BTFZ seismicity, which reaches at most 13–14 km on the BR (Kuna 2019; Lange 2026) and about 10 km at CAS/GD (Ren 2023). This conflicts with the stated shallow depth bias, so depth scatter or a datum problem in catalog v4 near the OBS network edge is likely. Treat the deep surfaces as artefacts until relocated.
  • Dips of 66–89° cannot separate vertical transform strands from the normal faults expected at CAS and SUR (about 45–60°; BN08 assumed 45°).

"Strike Δ" is the smallest angle between the surface strike and the reference strike, treating them as axial (0–90°). Reference geometries come from three sources: USGS ComCat moment tensors (MT) and first-motion solutions (FM) retrieved 2026-11-22; CRESCENT CFM trace azimuths computed from data/faults/crescent_cfm/crescent_cfm_crustal_traces.geojson; and published values. Mendocino fault (CFM trace) azimuth is 276/096. The trend between the 2024 Mw 7.0 epicentre and Punta Gorda is about 284/104. The offshore deformation front (CFM thrust AW_NCAL_2) is at about 124.8–124.95°W between 40.5 and 41°N. Surfaces with an empty slab2_minus_depth_km lie seaward of it, in the unsubducted Gorda plate.

Caveat that runs through all of this: catalog depths are biased shallow, with an unconfirmed datum. A surface 0–5 km "above" Slab2 may therefore sit at or inside the slab top. Dips of 15–35° on high-n, unstable surfaces (MT001–MT007) most likely describe seismic layers or volumes rather than single faults.

(a) Known structures (short overview)

Mendocino transform / Mendocino Fault Zone (MFZ). This is the right-lateral Pacific–Gorda transform. Offshore it trends about 096/276–104/284 along roughly 40.37–40.40°N, west of Cape Mendocino, and comes ashore near Punta Gorda (Furlong & Schwartz 2004; CFM). Characteristically repeating earthquakes show the easternmost (near-shore) MFZ creeping at about 65% of the long-term rate. They also show slower creep on a shallower Pacific–accretionary-wedge strike-slip interface and on a fault inside the Gorda plate (Materna et al. 2018). Shelly et al. (2026) note MFZ repeaters as shallow as ~10 km and others deeper than 20 km, next to the Gorda slab. Large recent MFZ events include the 1994 Mw 7.1, the 2010-02-04 M5.9 (MT 214/81/−6 or 305/84/−171; 124.96°W, 40.41°N), the 2021-12-20 offshore M6.1 (Yeck et al. 2023) and the 2024-12-05 Mw 7.0 (125.02°W, 40.37°N; MT 280/84 right-lateral). Hsu et al. (2026) find strike-slip-dominated stress in the MFZ, with compression highest close to the MTJ.

Southern Gorda plate and slab internal deformation. The Gorda plate is compressed N–S by the Pacific plate. Formerly ridge-parallel normal faults have rotated and are reactivated as NE-striking, left-lateral faults (Wilson 1989; Smith et al. 1993; Gulick et al. 2001; Chaytor et al. 2004; Ismat et al. 2022). The "Gorda deformation zone" has had 20 M≥5.9 events since 1976, on four fault orientations (Rollins & Stein 2010). Moment tensors of the large intraplate events in the 2010–2016 window have NE-striking left-lateral planes: 2010-01-10 Mw 6.5 (124.69°W, 40.65°N, 28.7 km; MT 53/84 or 57/80), 2014-03-10 Mw 6.8 (125.13°W, 40.83°N, 16 km; MT 228/82/−14) and 2016-09-03 M5.6 (N-striking, 187/86). The older 1980 Mw 7.2–7.3 Trinidad/Eureka event is similar (FM 51/89). Smith et al. (1993) show three things: no underthrusting of the Gorda plate along the MFZ (Pacific buttressing); NE-trending left-lateral faults; and a change from strike-slip to normal faulting with down-slab tension once the slab passes east of the triple junction (Hsu et al. 2026 agree).

Double seismic zone beneath Cape Mendocino and Humboldt Bay. There is a gently east-dipping double layer at 15–25 km (Wang & Rogers 1994; Smith et al. 1993). The upper layer is brittle slab crust. The lower layer is uppermost slab mantle. The 1992 M6.5/6.6 strike-slip aftershocks lay in the uppermost Gorda mantle (Velasco et al. 1994), and so did the onshore 2021 Petrolia M6.0/6.2 (Yoon & Shelly 2024). The 2022 Ferndale Mw 6.4 ruptured the uppermost slab, near the interface (Yoon & Shelly 2024). Shelly et al. (2024) describe it as left-lateral on an ENE-striking, near-vertical plane, with aftershocks running about 40 km ENE toward Fortuna. Aftershock families were (1) ENE left-lateral, (2) N-striking left-lateral and (3) normal. No aftershocks occurred on the interface. Guo et al. (2024) find the ENE fault confined to high-Vp/Vs slab crust and about 45° off the optimal orientation. The aftershocks overlapped the persistent "Fortuna cluster" (Alongi et al. 2021), a long-lived uppermost-slab cluster at about 17–24 km. Slab geometry references: McCrory et al. (2012), Hayes et al. (2018, Slab2), Gong & McGuire (2021), Guo et al. (2021).

1992 Cape Mendocino (Petrolia) Mw 7.2 and 1991 Honeydew M6.0. These were low-angle thrusts at about 10 km near Petrolia (MT 330/11/75, FM 352/13/90). The event was first read as subduction-interface rupture (Oppenheimer et al. 1993; Velasco et al. 1994). Later work placed it in the upper plate, above the slab (McCrory et al. 2012; Guo et al. 2021). Shelly et al. (2026) argue it ruptured the primary interface, which they place about 10 km above the petrologic slab at its southern end. The 1992 mainshock also produced a vigorous upper-plate aftershock sequence at about 10 km (Oppenheimer et al. 1993).

Southern edge of the Gorda slab, the "Garberville swarm" and Mendocino LFEs. Persistent microseismicity at about 22 km near Garberville has diverse strike-slip and normal mechanisms. It is the "Garberville swarm" (McPherson 1989; Castillo & Ellsworth 1993). About 15 km south of the southern limit of Gorda seismicity, low-frequency earthquakes align on a plane dipping NNE at 22–29 km (Shelly et al. 2025). The fit is strike 293°, dip 48° from hypocentres, and composite FM 292/34/177 (right-lateral strike-slip). Shelly et al. (2026) read this as the buried eastward extension of the Mendocino transform, the boundary of a captured "Pioneer" slab fragment (Furlong et al. 2024). They also propose a south-dipping southern edge to the Gorda slab.

Upper-plate faults (North American plate). The forearc fold-and-thrust belt (Little Salmon, Table Bluff, Mad River, McKinleyville, Fickle Hill, Trinidad) is made of NE-dipping reverse faults striking about 295–330° (Kelsey & Carver 1988; Clarke & Carver 1992; CFM). South and east of it are the San Andreas-system dextral faults: Garberville–Briceland (az ~315), Eaton Roughs / Lake Mountain (az ~330) and northern Maacama. Their seismicity ends near the surface projection of the slab's southern edge (Castillo & Ellsworth 1993; Kelsey & Carver 1988). Other structures nearby are the Russ fault (reverse-dextral, az 125/305), the Bear River fault (reverse, az 300) and the San Andreas, which comes ashore at Shelter Cove. NSHM23 fault sections follow the CFM/QFFD inventory (Petersen et al. 2024).

(c) Possible new or unrecognised structures

  1. MT020: an E–W, near-vertical alignment at about 40.73°N from 125.23 to 124.69°W, 20–32 km. It is parallel to the MFZ but 35 km north of it, and it crosses the deformation front. No published fault fits. It may be a real Gorda-plate structure (an MFZ-parallel right-lateral splay or a rotated fabric) or an artifact linking the 2010 and 2014 aftershock zones. Test it by relocating and checking focal mechanisms along the alignment.
  2. MT017: a NNW, vertical, 35 km-long zone between Rio Dell and Bridgeville, 7–26 km deep. It is parallel to the Eaton Roughs and Garberville–Briceland dextral faults but offset from their mapped traces. If its upper part (7–20 km) is crustal, it would be San Andreas-system seismicity north of the slab-edge projection, which is unusual (Castillo & Ellsworth 1993).
  3. MT021 / MT023: a NW-striking, NE-dipping zone in the unsubducted Gorda plate off Trinidad (about 41.05°N, 125.4°W). No known counterpart. The dips argue against a simple conjugate strike-slip plane.
  4. MT018: an NNE-striking, east-dipping (52°) surface at 0–15 km beneath Fortuna / Table Bluff, transverse to the NW-trending fold-thrust belt. Possibly a transverse fault or tear in the upper plate. Low n (121); check it.
  5. MT027: an ENE-striking, steep surface at 3–13 km at the Russ / Bear River / Garberville–Briceland junction. No mapped fault has this orientation. It may be long-lived 1992 upper-plate aftershock activity.
  6. MT015, MT025, MT019: individually plausible Gorda plate or slab faults (N–S strike-slip near the MFZ; N-striking slab normal faults). None corresponds to a named structure.

Location note: on the ridge, the v4 surfaces sit on the bathymetric axial valley. Teleseismic ComCat epicentres of ridge earthquakes plot about 10–20 km east of the axis (for example the 2008 M5.7, 2014 M5.5 and 2011 M5.2 events). Map positions in v4 therefore look better than ComCat's offshore. Absolute depths do not. Plane depths of 20–35 km near the Mendocino transform are far deeper than expected for 0–5 Myr lithosphere, so treat them with caution. Ren et al. (2025, EGU abstract) report OBS-located events down to about 40 km along the Mendocino transform, so deep seismicity there is not unique to v4.

(a) Known structures

1. Gorda Ridge (spreading centre, intermediate rate, about 55 mm/yr full rate). - Older work splits the ridge into three segments. More recent work, based on multibeam axis offsets, uses five; from north to south these are North Gorda, Jackson, Central, Phoenix and Escanaba (Chadwick et al., 1998, as cited by Clague et al., 2020). - North Gorda segment. About 65 km long, shallowest near 42.75°N. Site of the February–March 1996 dike intrusion and eruption, which SOSUS T-phases detected (Fox & Dziak, 1998; Chadwick et al., 1998; Clague et al., 2020). - Jackson segment. Lies south of North Gorda. NOAA PMEL detected a volcanic swarm there in 2001 (web archive, not peer reviewed). The President Jackson Seamounts, a 65 km volcanic chain starting near 42°20′N, sit off-axis to the west (Davis & Clague, 2000; Stoddard, 1989). - Escanaba Trough. Sediment-filled southernmost segment. It runs about 130 km north from the Mendocino ridge–transform intersection (RTI; 40°25′N, 127°30′W) to a right-lateral axis offset near 41°35′N. Its volcanic centres are SESCA (40°45′N) and NESCA (41°00′N) (Morton et al., 1987 USGS OFR; Zierenberg et al., 1993). - Faulting style. ComCat mechanisms on the axis show normal faulting with N–S to NNE strikes.

2. Gorda plate interior (Gorda Deformation Zone). - The plate deforms internally under N–S compression from the Pacific plate across the Mendocino transform (Silver, 1971; Riddihough, 1980; Wilson, 1986, 1989; Stoddard, 1987, 1991). - The accepted mechanism is left-lateral slip on NE-striking faults. These are spreading-fabric normal faults reactivated as strike-slip faults by flexural slip (Stoddard, 1987; Smith et al., 1993; Gulick et al., 2001; Chaytor et al., 2004). - Dziak et al. (2001) mapped this fabric with multibeam bathymetry. Chaytor et al. (2004) used multibeam bathymetry and T-phase seismicity. Secondary citations report that they found a second generation of strike-slip faults separating the northern and southern plate, and drag folding next to pseudofaults. - Hydroacoustic monitoring found a NNE-trending band of microseismicity through the centre of the plate in 1991–92 (Fox & Dziak, 1999). - Rollins & Stein (2010) count 20 M≥5.9 earthquakes since 1976 on four fault orientations. These include NE left-lateral faults, NW right-lateral faults (for example July 1991), the Mendocino transform and thrusts. - Velasco et al. (1994) used rupture directivity to show that the 17 August 1991 Mw 7.1 event (41.68°N, 125.86°W) ruptured a SW-striking fault, while the 13 July 1991 Mw 6.8 event (42.18°N, 125.64°W) ruptured a SE-striking one. Both orientations are active. - Ren et al. (2025, EGU abstract; independent PickBlue OBS catalog for 2012–2015) find that intraplate microseismicity follows spreading fabric and magnetic anomalies. Its northern limit lines up with pseudofault traces between 42°N and 42.5°N. - Ismat et al. (2022) describe the plate as a ductile deformation zone that shortens N–S and rotates clockwise. - Recent large events inside this region: - 2005-06-15 Mw 7.2 (41.29°N, 125.95°W). Nodal planes strike 317° and about 47°; it is interpreted as a NE left-lateral rupture, and its first-day aftershocks extend SW to about 40.8°N, 126.5°W. - 2005-06-17 Mw 6.6 (40.77°N, 126.57°W). Nodal plane strike 293°, so the conjugate strikes about 023°. - 2006 M5.0–5.1 events. - 2021-06-04 M5.9 doublet (42.27°N, 126.25°W; strike about 052°). - 2025-09-09 M5.7 (42.36°N, 126.56°W; strike 055°). - The 1980 Mw 7.2 Trinidad/Eureka, 2010 M6.5, 2014 M6.8 and 2021/2022 events are inside the excluded triple-junction box. Guo et al. (2024) cover the 2022 event and Hsu et al. (2026) cover the triple-junction region.

3. Mendocino transform (right-lateral, Pacific–Gorda), from the RTI at about 127.5°W eastward. - Strike-slip mechanisms strike 265°–275° (or 087°–095°): 1996 M5.5, 1997 M5.4, 2008 M5.9, 2009, 2015-04-24 M5.4, 2015-04-10 M4.7 and 2016-12-08 Mw 6.6 (40.45°N, 126.19°W; strike 95°). The 2018 M5.8 has nodal planes at 271° and 181°. - Sverdrup (1987) relocated earthquakes near the RTI. - Hsu et al. (2026) find background rate and aftershock productivity on the transform decrease westward with distance from the triple junction. - Chen & McGuire (2016) measured source parameters with the 2012–2013 Cascadia Initiative OBS array. A 2014 AGU abstract by the same authors reports lower stress drops near the plate boundary than inside the Gorda plate; the 2016 paper itself was not read.

4. Margin near Brookings. Stone et al. (2018) note abundant offshore OBS seismicity south of 43°N but name no structure off Brookings.

(c) Possible new or under-documented structures

  1. WNW right-lateral source zone near 41.85°N, 126.1°W (GO010, GO011, GO017).
  2. The seismicity planes strike 114–124°, which favours the WNW plane rather than the NE left-lateral faults that dominate the Gorda models.
  3. The site has produced M4–5.7 strike-slip events repeatedly: 2006, 2014, 2016, 2019, 2020 and 2026 (M5.7).
  4. I found no named fault for it. It may be a member of the NW-trending right-lateral set (the 1991 July type), or a conjugate cross-fault in a fabric-parallel system.
  5. Relocating the 2026 M5.7 sequence would test this.
  6. Brookings offshore swarm, 2012 (GO020).
  7. Near-slab-interface swarm on the inner margin, recorded by ANSS and v4. I found no publication.
  8. Worth examining as possible slab or interface seismicity, or as a fluid-driven burst.
  9. Oblique transfer fault at the Escanaba–Central offset (GO012), about 41.6°N. The offset itself is documented; a seismically active oblique fault crossing it is not.
  10. SE-bending southern Escanaba axis at the RTI (GO005, GO007, GO009).
  11. Normal faults striking about 335° rather than N–S at 40.5–40.8°N.
  12. The RTI geometry is mapped (Sverdrup, 1987, relocations; USGS sidescan sonar), but no paper ties a NNW-trending active fault zone there to the seismicity.
  13. Deep (20–35 km) seismicity at the RTI and on the transform (GO001, GO004–GO009). Independently noted by Ren et al. (2025, EGU abstract: mantle shear zones along the Mendocino transform). It needs relocation before interpretation because of v4's absolute depth bias.

Pseudofaults and propagator scars: no v4 surface follows a pseudofault trace. The 42–42.5°N pseudofault limit of Ren et al. (2025) has seismicity in ComCat (2021 M5.9 doublet, 2025 M5.7, both NE-striking), but those events fall after the catalog period.

(a) Overview

What the set contains. All 30 surfaces are crustal. The deepest bottom edge is 28.6 km, and slab2_minus_depth ranges from +3.6 to +69 km. None of the surfaces is intraslab. The deep Juan de Fuca Wadati–Benioff seismicity under Puget Sound, which hosted the 1949, 1965 and 2001 Nisqually events at 50–60 km depth (Ichinose et al., 2004; McCrory et al., 2012), produced no surface in this group. The St. Helens Seismic Zone (Weaver & Smith, 1983) lies south of the Puget box, so it belongs to the onshore group. No megathrust surface was found offshore Washington. The only surface near the slab top is WA005, and it is steep.

Strong matches. - PU011 matches the NNE-dipping seismic zone that Li et al. (2018) relocated beneath the eastern 30 km of the Leech River fault (LRF), which extends about 20 km east offshore. PU011 covers the same along-strike extent and dips 44° N. The CFM gives the LRF a 52° N dip, and the 2016 AGU abstract by the same group gave about 45°. Strike difference is about 9°. - PU022 is the volcano-tectonic cluster beneath the Mount Rainier summit (Moran et al., 2000). - PU019 lies on the Rattlesnake Mountain fault zone, at the SE end of the SWIF system (Sherrod et al., 2008), and runs to the 1996 M5.3 Duvall source area.

Medium matches. - PU003 is the Western Rainier Seismic Zone (WRSZ). Stanley et al. (1996) describe it as a north-trending band west of the park, about 65 km long, with en echelon oblique faults. - PU016 is at the SE end of the Olympia structure, an 80 km long, 315°-trending potential-field lineament (Clement et al., 2010). Strike agrees within about 5°. - PU025 is on the SWIF main strand at Possession Sound (Johnson et al., 1996). - PU018 and PU015 are E–W and north-dipping, along the inferred eastern continuation of the Tacoma fault. Johnson et al. (2004) describe the Tacoma fault as a north-dipping backthrust. Brocher et al. (2004) describe a north-dipping roof thrust of the Seattle uplift. - PU012 and PU009 follow the western Seattle fault zone, the Hood Canal area and the Seattle–Saddle Mountain transfer zone (Blakely et al., 2002, 2009; Lamb et al., 2012).

Recurring polarity mismatch. Many Puget surfaces dip the opposite way from the mapped master faults: - Seattle fault and Olympia structure: S-dipping in the CFM. - SWIF: NE-dipping in the CFM. - Devils Mountain fault: N-dipping in the CFM.

The well-documented exceptions are the north-dipping backthrusts: Toe Jam Hill (Nelson et al., 2003), Tacoma (Johnson et al., 2004) and the roof thrust (Brocher et al., 2004). Where a surface's dip is 20–35°, the input caveat applies: the plane may be fitting a seismogenic layer rather than a fault.

Van Wagoner et al. (2002) found that Puget Lowland earthquakes sit mostly in Crescent Formation basement, while the sedimentary basins and the eastern Olympic subduction complex are nearly aseismic. They also found that hypocenters are hard to tie to individual mapped faults. Several broad, low-dip surfaces here (PU001, PU005, PU002, PU013, PU010) are best read as this basement seismicity rather than as faults.

Interpretation to test. PU002 dips 27° E. Its sea-level projection, at about 123.25–123.35°W, falls near the eastern Olympic contact between the Crescent Formation and the Olympic subduction complex (Hurricane Ridge fault; Brandon et al., 1998). PU002 may therefore mark the western, east-dipping edge of seismogenic Crescent basement, not a Quaternary fault.

(a) Overview

St. Helens seismic zone (SHZ). - The SHZ is a 90–100 km NNW-trending zone of dextral strike-slip, with N–S preferred nodal planes (Weaver & Smith 1983). - Mount St. Helens sits at a dextral offset in the zone, with local extension between the fault segments (Weaver et al. 1987). - Stanley et al. (1996) add three relevant points: - The SHZ is near vertical close to the volcano but dips steeply west farther north. It may merge with a band of seismicity at about 18 km depth in the lower Siletzia crust. - An ENE band of seismicity at 12–20 km depth, north of the Cowlitz River, steps over from the SHZ to the western Rainier zone. - A NE-trending zone at about 18 km depth runs from the SHZ toward Portland. - Stanley et al. (1996) also name the Goat Rocks zone, a discontinuous NNW alignment of epicenters running north-northwest from Goat Rocks. - v4 resolves all of these features: ON001/ON003, ON002/ON005, ON004/ON008 and ON018. - The v4 dips of 35–39° W on ON001/ON003 have the same sense as the "steep westward dip" in Stanley et al. (1996) but are much shallower. A broad zone of seismicity, plus the deep band it merges with, probably flattens the fitted planes.

Mount Hood. - Swarms on the south flank are regarded as tectonic (Jones & Malone 2005). - The N-trending Mount Hood fault zone, including the Twin Lakes and Multorpor segments, has been mapped from lidar (Madin et al. 2021). - ON015 matches the Twin Lakes fault closely: 2.5 km away, ΔS = 4°, dip 55° against 50° in the CFM.

Portland basin. - Yelin & Patton (1991) tied sparse seismicity at 15–20 km depth to the Portland Hills and Frontal fault zones. - Blakely et al. (2000) and Liberty et al. (2003) map the NW-striking Portland Hills, Sylvan–Oatfield and East Bank faults. - ON013 (Portland Hills north, ΔS = 4°) and ON031 (Portland Hills south / Bolton, ΔS = 4–5°) agree in strike. Both sit at 15–18 km depth, the same depth range Yelin & Patton (1991) reported. - The Mount Angel fault (Thomas et al. 1996) and the Gales Creek fault (Horst et al. 2020) have no strong v4 counterpart.

North Cascades foothills, Fraser Lowland and San Juan Islands. - Literature for this area: - Kelsey et al. (2012): Bellingham basin faults. - Sherrod et al. (2013): Boulder Creek–Canyon Creek fault. - Balfour et al. (2012): double-difference lineations in SW British Columbia and the San Juan Islands. - Greene et al. (2018): the active, steep, left-lateral Skipjack Island fault zone, striking WNW. - ON011 (WNW, 60° dip, about 13 km depth) is the best San Juan candidate. ON009 and ON010 are parallel planes fitted to the same cluster.

Offshore Oregon. - Goldfinger et al. (1992, 1997) mapped WNW left-lateral forearc faults (Daisy Bank, Wecoma, Alvin Canyon) and N–S anticlines (Stonewall Bank). - Seismicity beneath the shelf near 44.3–44.6°N has been interpreted as plate-boundary thrusting, possibly above subducted seamounts (Tréhu et al. 2008, 2012, 2015; Williams et al. 2011). - OR001 and OR002 fall in this cluster. No surface lies on Daisy Bank or Wecoma.

Endeavour–Explorer. - The 2014 Mw 6.4 Nootka sequence ruptured a new fault in the subducted Explorer plate that strikes about 156° and is more than 55 km long (Hutchinson et al. 2020, 2023). EN003 (strike 344° = 164°) contains only April–May 2014 events, so it is this fault. - EN004 lies 1.7 km from the CFM "Southern Nootka" trace, with ΔS = 8°. The primary faults of the Nootka fault zone strike NE (Hyndman et al. 1979; Rohr et al. 2018; Hutchinson et al. 2023). - EN002 lies in the broad conjugate-fault deformation zone of the eastern Sovanco fracture zone (Braunmiller & Nábělek 2002). - EN001 is a ridge-parallel structure at the southern end of the Endeavour segment, where the Cobb propagating rift overlaps it (Krauss et al. 2023).

Targets with no v4 surface. None of the following produced a surface, mostly because they were quiet in 2010–2016 or seismicity there fell below the clustering thresholds: Leech River fault (Morell et al. 2017, 2018), Klamath Falls 1993 (Braunmiller et al. 1995), the western Rainier zone itself, Three Sisters, Newberry, Crater Lake, Entiat/Chelan, Beaufort Range, Nanoose, Daisy Bank and Wecoma.

Outside the named region. The ON set also includes surfaces near Redding, California (ON030, ON040).
