# Blanco Transform Fault Zone: literature match for catalog-v4 fault surfaces BL001–BL027

Input: `faults_blanco.csv` (27 surfaces). Method: each centroid and top-edge was projected onto a reference line through the BTFZ–Juan de Fuca ridge-transform intersection (RTI) at 44.45°N, 130.4°W (the origin used by Braunmiller & Nábělek 2008, Fig. 15), azimuth 111° (plate-motion trend 291°, Wilson 1993). "along" = km SE of the RTI; "perp" = km off that line (+ = north). Strikes are compared modulo 180°. Dips are not used as evidence (see caveats).

## (a) Known structures along the BTFZ (W to E)

Total length about 350 km. Right-lateral (dextral) motion at about 51–56 mm/yr, plate-motion azimuth about 291° (Wilson 1993; Braunmiller & Nábělek 2008). Embley & Wilson (1992) mapped five transform segments separated by deep basins using SeaBeam. Braunmiller & Nábělek (2008, BN08) grouped them into four seismotectonic segments. Ren et al. (2023) and Lange et al. (2026; arXiv preprint 2503.17148) used the 2012–13 Blanco OBS experiment (X9, 54–55 OBS) to resolve individual strands.

### Duplicate / overlap groups

- **Blanco Ridge:** BL003 (main) = BL013 (W, collinear) ⊃ BL019 (E asperity). BL023 is the deep (mantle) image. BL024 is a deep artefact at the W tip. BL011 is the N-shifted W end next to CAS.
- **Cascadia Depression:** BL005 ≈ BL008 ≈ BL012 ≈ BL021 are all N–S to NNW planes in one basin; they may be several normal faults, or one cluster split into duplicates. BL007 and BL022 are oblique or deep composites of the same cluster.
- **Surveyor–EBD:** BL001 ⊃ BL006, BL009, BL020 (SUR strands); BL002 (EBD) overlaps BL001/BL009. BL010 is the oblique step.
- **WBD / RTI:** BL004 ⊃ BL015. BL018 runs parallel to BL015, about 4 km N. BL027 and BL025 are the ridge-ward curvature and the Cleft segment.
- **Gorda Depression:** BL016 (N wall) ∥ BL014 (interior / S trough) ∥ BL017 (GT, S wall). BL026 is the deep artefact.

## (c) Possible new or unmapped structures

1. **BL025 (Cleft segment, 44.6–44.84°N):** a coherent, stable, ridge-parallel seismic plane about 20 km N of the RTI. The OBS studies (2012–13) describe the JdFR as nearly aseismic. If BL025 does not come from one transient episode (check event times), it is a seismogenic structure that has not been reported on the southern Cleft segment.
2. **BL010 (EBD–SUR oblique cross fault, strike 143°, dip about 66°):** no named fault at this spot. It may be a pull-apart cross-basin normal or transtensional fault. Worth checking against SeaBeam lineaments (Dauteuil 1995).
3. **BL014 (structure inside the GD / S of the BR):** possibly the trough fault BN08 proposed from a few strike-slip events. If confirmed, it would be a third parallel strand between the GD N wall (BL016) and the GT (BL017).
4. **BL011 (BR–CAS junction, 54° dip, N-shifted):** this matches the seismicity–bathymetry misalignment Lange reported and the NE fault bend Journeau reported, but no one has mapped a fault plane there. A moderately dipping transtensional link would be new geometry, provided the dip survives relocation.
5. **Not recovered:** no surface lies on the Parks Plateau Fault or the north-of-BR lineament near 43.5°N 127.25°W (BN08 §45), and none on Lange's off-axis cluster at 44.4°N 129.68°W. Their absence is consistent with the literature, which describes these as nearly aseismic or as very short bursts.

### Caveats for interpretation

- Strikes of 120–124° on the long western surfaces (BL001, BL002, BL006, BL009) are about 10–13° clockwise of plate motion. That is the signature of an envelope across right-stepping en-echelon strands, not the strike of an individual fault. The OBS-resolved strands are parallel to plate motion.
- Surfaces whose bottom lies below about 15 km (BL017, BL022–BL024, BL026; also BL005/007/008/012 at 25–30 km) go deeper than any published BTFZ seismicity, which reaches at most 13–14 km on the BR (Kuna 2019; Lange 2026) and about 10 km at CAS/GD (Ren 2023). This conflicts with the stated shallow depth bias, so depth scatter or a datum problem in catalog v4 near the OBS network edge is likely. Treat the deep surfaces as artefacts until relocated.
- Dips of 66–89° cannot separate vertical transform strands from the normal faults expected at CAS and SUR (about 45–60°; BN08 assumed 45°).
