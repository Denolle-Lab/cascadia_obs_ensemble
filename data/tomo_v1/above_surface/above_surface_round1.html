Events above the ground or seafloor

Root causes, round 1 · 2026-11-24 · four investigations, each checked by an independent verifier who tried to refute it

The rule (D-B055): no event above the solid surface goes into a published catalog, and such an event is a sign of a wrong computation, to be fixed where it happens rather than moved. The surface is GMT earth_relief 01m, interpolated bilinearly. Three sets of events break the rule today, and they have different causes.

1. Catalog v4: the location steps never knew where the surface is

Our repository did not change any v4 depth: utils/fix_catalog_v4.py touched 21 dates and nothing else. The depths are Ian's. In the public GENIE code (commit e6f3a1d) topography is off by default, the source search domain extends above sea level, and the travel-time grid fills the air and the water with the velocity of the top layer of the 1D model. GraphDD has no surface or depth bound at all. In Ian's 2025 GENIE run the domain reached 5 km above sea level: 217 events sit exactly at a depth of −5.000 km, and 37 % of the GENIE-stage events were already above the surface before GraphDD ran.

Axial carries half of the problem: 18,878 of its 26,731 events (71 %) are above the seafloor. Ian's model there (data/vel_pnsn_jdf.csv) starts with Vp 6.5 km/s at sea level, with rock in the water column, about twice the speed of Axial's shallow crust. The v4 positions fit the picks best with exactly that velocity. With all eight stations on one seafloor plane, a hypocentre and its mirror image above the plane fit the picks equally well (within 0.02 s for all 18,612 tested events), so nothing kept the events below the seafloor. Away from Axial, the events above the surface are the shallow tail of a large vertical error (interquartile range 3 to 7 km) on poorly constrained events: few S picks, only seafloor stations, large spatial sigma.

No change of depth datum rescues v4. Taking depths relative to the local surface or to the station elevations still leaves 16 to 19 % above, and makes the agreement with ANSS worse.

Consequence. The v4 and v4.1 depths cannot be published as they are, and the paper uses v4.1. The hypocentres have to come from a location that has the solid Earth in its model and a surface in its search, which is what our NLLoc relocation does. For Ian, if he reruns: topography on with a real DEM and bathymetry, a water layer, the search top at the highest topography, densely sampled 1D profiles referenced to the seafloor offshore, and a surface bound in GraphDD.

2. Our NLLoc relocation: a coarse surface lookup, now fixed

NLLoc reads its topography file at the nearest grid node, while the check interpolates bilinearly. On slopes the nearest node can sit higher than the true surface under the epicentre, so NLLoc accepted locations above it. Reproducing NLLoc's lookup explains every case: 1,079 of 1,079 in B4 and 594 of 594 in Track A.

The fix needs no code change (D-B056, branch reloc/track-a 85cc3dae). A new 15″ file holds, at each node, the lowest point of the bilinear surface over the area NLLoc assigns to that node, minus 1 m, in the Earth-flattened frame. Tests: 0 events above the surface among 500 sampled, 1,078 B4 and 594 Track A affected events; 197 of 200 random controls did not move, and the other 3 moved by one search cell. The v0.3.1 relocation uses it from its first pass (about 11:00 today).

open The verifier found a deeper problem. Rerun without the surface, the fit puts about 4 % of B4 events (6,500; 29.5 % of JdF ridge, 18.9 % of Endeavour, 7 % of Axial) 0.3 to 1.7 km above the surface. For these events the surface sets the depth, not the data, which amounts to the clamping you rejected. One concrete suspect: a1_build_model.py line 253 places 590 of 696 stations about 50 m below the real surface, against its own docstring. Round 2 is testing this and the near-surface model.

4. Decisions for you

Paper catalog. I recommend making the NLLoc relocation in the final model the paper's catalog once the round-2 fixes are in, with v4 kept as the association and pick catalog. Asking Ian for a corrected GENIE/GraphDD run is useful as a cross-check but not needed for the hypocentres.

Events that cannot be located in 3D (a few thousand after the fixes, estimated): list them with the v4 epicentre flagged as such and no depth, or leave them out of the hypocentre table.
