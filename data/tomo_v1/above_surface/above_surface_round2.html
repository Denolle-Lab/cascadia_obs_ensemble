The upward pull and the station reach

Root causes, round 2 · 2026-11-24 · two investigations, each checked by an independent verifier who reran the tests on new samples · round 1: above_surface_round1.html

Round 1 fixed NLLoc's coarse topography lookup (D-B056): the v0.3.1 relocation has no located event above the surface. It left two questions. Why does the fit pull about 5 % of the events up against the surface? And why do far stations drop out of the locations? Round 2 answers most of both. Neither fix is ready to apply as it stands.

1. The upward pull

The travel-time grids are not the problem. A trilinear lookup reproduces NLLoc's predicted times to 0.3 ms, and synthetic arrivals computed in the same grids are located back within 0.1–0.2 km for well-recorded events. Station placement (most stations sit about 50 m below the surface, a consistency error but not a cause), station terms, the near-station correction, the S-pick quality rule, rock or water above the surface, and a slow top kilometre for S were each tested one at a time and none removes the pull.

The misfit setting

Our template uses LOCGAU2 0.02 0.05 2.0: the modelling error grows with the predicted travel time (2 %, between 0.05 and 2 s). Inside the EDT misfit, NLLoc weights each pair of arrivals by 1/√(si² + sj²) and raises the sum to the number of arrivals, so shorter predicted times get larger weights. For events with poor depth resolution this works like a prior toward shallow positions close to the stations. With perfect synthetic data placed 0.5 km below the surface, 7 of 50 such events end 0.25 to 2.6 km above it; without LOCGAU2, 1 of 50. With a 200,000-node search the same 7 events still end above, so the search is not the cause. On real data, removing LOCGAU2 lets 11 of 50 floor-held events leave the floor, and 8 of them move to 17–55 km depth with an RMS of 7–91 ms instead of 148–389 ms. Those deep solutions are probably not right at the ridges either. The setting changes which depth the likelihood prefers when the data cannot choose, and that choice should be made on purpose.

Late S arrivals

The pulled events have S arrivals late relative to P, by a median 0.49 s in B4 (Track A 1.1 s). A shared delay of 0.15 to 0.3 s is enough to lift a shallow source to the surface, even a well-resolved one. The delay is not uniform: it grows with distance (0.29 s within 20 km, 0.5–0.6 s beyond), and in 16 of 63 events the S residuals have both signs. This points to wrong S picks or associations, wrong epicentres on one-sided networks, or a Vp/Vs bias of 2–3 %, rather than one delay per event. A waveform check of a few events would tell picks from model.

Which events, and a flag

In v0.3.1 iter0, 9,481 events (6.0 %) lie within one search cell of the D-B056 floor. Their depth uncertainty is large (σz 9.3 km, the expectation depth 13 km below the best point): the floor, not the data, sets their depth. They are 44 % of JdF ridge and Endeavour events, 7 % of Blanco and 6 % of Axial. A floor_limited flag can be computed from the production output with no rerun. It must test against the floor NLLoc actually uses, and the pile-up extends to about 1.5 search cells.

The atlas showed a related point: under 725 of these events the GMRT multibeam seafloor is lower than earth_relief 01m (median 41 m, up to 0.5 km), so with the better bathymetry they sit in the water. The floor and the test should use the best bathymetry available.

Track A

a1_build_model.py makes a node solid only when the node itself is below the surface, while Grid2Time applies each node to the cell below it, so up to 1 km of water or air lies below the true surface; 6,108 Track A events are located inside it, invisible to the above-surface test. It also samples velocities at cell corners, which shifts the whole Track A structure by half a cell. B4 samples cell centres and is not affected. Track A only needs fixing if it is used again.

2. Station reach, the 80 km floor, five-pick events

Wide fallback grids. A second, coarse (2 km) travel-time grid per station and phase covering the whole model, listed after the fine grid, lets NLLoc use every arrival. On 455 test events, dropped arrivals fell from 6,043 to 0 and located events rose from 249 to 413, including 107 of the 146 M ≥ 4 events that had kept v4 positions; 134 of 149 control events came out identical. Before production: the wide grids disagree with the fine ones by −0.36 to +0.62 s per station-phase (91 of 222 beyond ±0.1 s), probably because their near-source correction differs, and station terms would mix the two; chunks with many far arrivals run 3 to 14 times longer (+7–12 % overall); the run must count every dropped arrival, not only logged rejections; and reruns need new run names. Newly located events seen only by distant stations get poor depths (median difference from ANSS 21–24 km instead of 11; Blanco M5–6 at 59–77 km) and need their own flag.

Five-pick events. 12,108 events have exactly 5 picks, the v4 minimum. With a 5-phase minimum, 12,044 locate, none above the surface, nearly all tier D (one degree of freedom; σz 13 km, horizontal error 27 km).

80 km floor. A deep pass with wide grids extended to 121 km located 16 of 17 well-recorded intraslab events at 78–107 km and left 10 control events within 0.35 km. It needs velocities below 80 km outside the tomography box, which the models do not have.

Tier U in the catalog. Patches stop the catalog builders from copying v4 hypocentres. They must be merged with catalog_version.py for v5.1, since v5.0 keeps v4 hypocentres by your choice (D-B059).

3. Recommended next steps

  1. Test the misfit: rerun the floor-held sample and controls with LOCGAU2 off and with a constant model error, and score the depths against Hirao (Mount St Helens), Lange (Blanco) and Axial.
  2. Check the S picks of a few pulled events on the waveforms.
  3. Correct the wide grids so they agree with the fine grids, build them for v0.3.1, add the five-pick and bathymetry choices, and rerun iter0/iter1 under new run names for v5.1.
  4. Publish floor_limited and a far-stations-only flag in v5.1; drop v4 hypocentres from tier U.
