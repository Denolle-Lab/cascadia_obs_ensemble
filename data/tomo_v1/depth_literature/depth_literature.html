Earthquake depths by region: our catalog against the literature

Cascadia OBS ensemble catalog, 2010–2016, 172,340 events. Compares B4 (after the tomography) and v4-NLL3D (before) with verified published depth constraints. Draft for the PI and co-authors, 24 November 2026. Scratch analysis only: no repository file or catalog was changed.

Conventions. Depths are in km. bsl = below sea level, positive down. Below surface = below the local seafloor offshore (bsf) or below the ground onshore, computed as depth + surface_km, where surface_km is the local elevation, positive up. Ranges are p10–p90 around the median (p50). A-D = all located events; A-C drops tier D; tiers A-B have depth constrained by a nearby station. v4-NLL3D (NLL3D below) is NonLinLoc in the 3D starting model, before the tomography. B4 is NonLinLoc in the merged tomoDD model v0.3.0 with new station terms, after the tomography. “Offset” is B4 minus the literature: positive means our events are deeper.

In short

  • B4 is too deep at Axial Seamount by about 2–2.5 km below the seafloor, on the same events and stations that Waldhauser et al. (2020) located. The cause is the v0.3.0 model under the caldera, where Vp/Vs falls to 1.35–1.40. NLL3D agreed with the literature there; the tomography moved events away from it.
  • On the Endeavour, Explorer and Juan de Fuca plates the depth is not constrained (median sig_z about 11 km, nearest station 28–58 km). B4 puts many of these events 15–45 km below the seafloor, under a crust only 6–10 km thick. These depths should not be reported.
  • B4 is also too deep in the Gorda interior (+8 to +12 km at the median, +3 to +6 km near an OBS), on the Blanco Ridge (+3 to +5 km) and at Mendocino (+2 to +4 km against NCSN and the slab literature). The tomography reduced the Gorda and Blanco offsets. The Mendocino offset was already in NLL3D.
  • B4 agrees with the literature within about 1 km in Puget/Georgia, at the offshore-forearc benchmarks (Fortuna, Newport), in Oregon and SW Washington, and at Mount St Helens.
  • The depth datum does not explain the offsets. Where the literature gives depth below the seafloor we convert with surface_km; at Axial that conversion is good to about 0.1 km. Onshore and on the coast the surface is within 0.5 km of sea level.
  • Next: QC the merged model (reject Vp/Vs below about 1.6 and nodes with no resolution), add depth-quality flags, and run the diagnostic relocations and event-level benchmarks listed in Overall findings before GrowClust3D (B5).

2. Axial Seamount too deep confidence: high

B4 puts Axial caldera events about 2–2.5 km deeper than the published catalogs of the same events. NLL3D was close to the literature. The tomography moved the depths away from it.

What the literature says

Datum: below the seafloor (caldera floor). The caldera floor is at 1.5 km bsl (Levy et al. 2018).

  • Wilcock et al. (2016): about 60,000 events before the April 2015 eruption, located by double difference. Outward-dipping ring faults (60–70°) run from near the surface to about 2 km, with about five times more events under the east wall. Fig. 1C shows a sparse east-wall tail to 2.5–2.9 km. The datum is read from the figure axis, where 0 km follows the caldera floor. Tan et al. (2019), on the same catalog: depths mostly less than 2 km.
  • Waldhauser et al. (2020), real-time double difference, 22 Jan 2015 to 31 Jan 2017, same cabled array: 85 % of events within the network lie above 1.6 km and 90 % above 2 km. The faults reach the top of the magma reservoir at about 1.6 km. Absolute NonLinLoc depth errors are about 800 m (median).
  • Levy et al. (2018): seismicity before, during and after the eruption lies mostly above about 2.5 km, with sparse events to about 2.8 km under the eastern chamber roof.
  • West et al. (2001): caldera microearthquakes do not extend deeper than 2 km.
  • Magma reservoir roof: 1.1–2.3 km (Arnulf et al. 2014), 1.1–2.0 km (Carbotte et al. 2020), about 1.7 km (Yang et al. 2024). The melt body is up to 1 km thick. The largest melt fraction is at 2.5–3.5 km (West et al. 2001). A stacked-sill conduit reaches about 5 km (Carbotte et al. 2020).
  • Not verified: Baillard et al. (2019), the 3-D Vp/Vs model and relocation of more than 76,000 events; datum not confirmed. Arnulf et al. (2018).

Verdict and reasoning

Too deep by about +2 to +2.5 km at the median (at least +1.8 km), and +2.8 to +2.9 km at p85/p90. This is a like-for-like comparison: same events, same stations, same years. Magnitude bias and different structures play no part.

B4 puts 77 % of located events below 2 km bsf. Its median of 3.4 km (3.8 km for A-C) sits inside or under the melt reservoir, where brittle failure is not expected. The error is systematic. The best-constrained B4 events are the deepest: the sig_z < 1 km subset (n = 1,243) has a median of 3.95 km and 95 % of it lies below 2 km. Events within 1 km of a station have a median of 3.83 km, so station geometry is not the cause. B4 does not fit the data better (median RMS 0.060 s against 0.054 s). Its tiers collapsed: A plus B fell from 13,144 to 1,286 events, and median sig_z rose from 1.18 to 1.83 km. Epicentres moved too, by a median 2.7 km and mostly north. The east-to-west event ratio across the caldera fell from 9.6 (NLL3D) to 2.8 (B4), against about 5 in Wilcock et al. (2016). exp_depth makes it worse (3.86 km bsf).

Cause: the v0.3.0 model under the caldera. It comes from the ridges_north_m0/d200_s60 tile (smoothing 60, against 480 for the onshore tiles). At the node 46.0°N, 130.0°W, 3 km bsl, Vp fell from 5.40 to 4.79 km/s while Vs rose from 3.16 to 3.43 km/s. That gives Vp/Vs = 1.40, and 1.35 at 5 km. Both are below √2, which implies a negative Poisson’s ratio, impossible for isotropic rock. S coverage is thin (dws_s 77–236 against dws_p 665–1,610). In the NonLinLoc grids, the S−P time per km of path beneath the caldera dropped from 0.30 to 0.11–0.18 s/km at 1–2 km grid depth, and from 0.13 to 0.09 s/km at 3–4 km. With a station almost overhead, the same observed S−P time then needs a path 1.5–2 times longer. That matches the shift from about 1.5 to about 3.5 km bsf.

NLL3D has its own limits. 10 % of its located events sit at one grid node (3.006 km bsl, about 1.46 km bsf). Its starting model has no magma-chamber low-velocity zone. Its A-B p90 is 2.39 km against 2.0 km in the literature. This small deep tail is within the 800 m absolute error that Waldhauser et al. (2020) report for NonLinLoc at Axial.

Effect of the tomography

Away from the literature, by about 2 km. Paired B4 − NLL3D over the same 26,371 events: median +1.82 km (p10 −0.77, p90 +2.99). On the 6,599 events tier A-C in both: +2.36 km. For the 13,144 events that are tier A-B in NLL3D, the median moves from 1.48 to 3.62 km bsf, and only 1,272 of them stay A-B in B4.

Recommended action

  1. Do not adopt B4 hypocentres at Axial, epicentres included. Keep the v4-NLL3D locations for this region for now.
  2. Fix v0.3.0 before re-running B4 here. In the merge or QC gate, reject nodes with Vp/Vs below about 1.6 and require a minimum dws_s; where either test fails, revert Vs to the starting model. The merged model has 57 nodes in the upper 10 km with Vp/Vs < 1.5 and some S coverage. Besides Axial (45.9–46.0°N, 129.9–130.0°W, 3–5 km) they include Endeavour (48.0°N, 129.0–129.1°W, 1–5 km), Gorda/Mendocino (41.2–41.3°N, 125.25–125.38°W, 3–7 km) and the Blanco area (44.2–44.4°N).
  3. Re-run the ridges_north inversion with stronger smoothing, or with Vs damped more than Vp.
  4. Check the new S station terms at the Axial stations.
  5. Match our events one by one with the Waldhauser et al. (2020) catalog for 2015–2016. It uses the same stations, so matching on origin time gives the depth offset directly.
  6. Acceptance test for any new model: at least 85 % of A-B Axial events above 2 km bsf. NLL3D has 79 %, B4 12 %.

3. Endeavour too deep depth unconstrained confidence: high

Neither catalog constrains depth here. NLL3D pins half the events at the seafloor; B4 pushes most of them 15–45 km down, below a crust that is 6–8 km thick. Do not report Endeavour depths.

What the literature says

Datum: below the seafloor in every study. The seafloor is 2.1–2.75 km bsl.

  • Wilcock et al. (2002), 1995 OBS experiment (55 days, 1,750 events): axial earthquakes concentrate at 1.5–3.5 km. Relocated events near the seismic lines lie at 1.5–2.7 km (Van Ark et al. 2007).
  • Van Ark et al. (2007): the axial magma chamber (AMC) reflector is at 2.1–3.3 km; 2.1–2.4 km under the vent fields and 2.5–3.1 km under Mothra. Most axial earthquakes lie just above it.
  • Wilcock et al. (2009), 2003–2004 network: hypocentres form a layer a few hundred metres thick above or beside the AMC, about 1.2–3.0 km (read from Fig. 2).
  • Weekly et al. (2013), 2003–2006: a band about 1 km thick above the AMC. Events more than 3 km from the nearest station were fixed at 3 km depth because depth is not resolved there.
  • Krauss et al. (2023), 3-D NonLinLoc relocation: vent-field medians of 0.8–1.9 km; the constrained-depth ONC events (2016–2021) lie mostly above 2 km.
  • Arnoux et al. (2017): a low-velocity zone at 2–3 km under the vent fields.
  • Crustal thickness 5.9–7.7 km (Soule et al. 2016) and 6.4–7.6 km (Arnoux et al. 2019) across the whole 90 × 120 km region.

Verdict and reasoning

Too deep, but not as a calibrated offset. Against a reference of about 2 km bsf, B4 is about +25 km too deep for A-D and +14 km for A-C. With one tight station pair and everything else 50–130 km away, depth trades off freely with origin time. Both catalogs land on boundaries: NLL3D at the seafloor, B4 either at the seafloor (19 % of A-D) or 25–45 km deep. Earthquakes 15–55 km deep are physically implausible anywhere in this region, on axis or at the segment ends, because the crust is 6–8 km thick and the lithosphere is young.

Likely cause, inferred and not tested: v0.3.0 has zero resolution (resolution_p = 0) at every depth from 1 to 12 km in the box 47.5–48.4°N, 129.6–128.8°W, with dws_s of 55 or less. It still carries perturbations of −15 to +19 % in Vp and −8 to +16 % in Vs, and Vp/Vs as low as 1.28–1.47. At the node nearest the vent fields (48.0°N, 129.125°W) Vp/Vs is 1.47–1.56 at 1–5 km bsl. Weekly et al. (2013) used 1.72 below 0.4 km. The B4 S station terms also grew: KEMF +0.21 s (NLL3D +0.03), NCHR +0.34 s (+0.14), J55C +0.32 s (+0.04). A too-fast S model shortens the predicted S−P time at KEMF and NCHR and pushes hypocentres away from them, here downward.

Effect of the tomography

Away from the literature. Paired B4 − NLL3D: median +6.8 km (p90 +37 km) for A-D and +7.4 km for A-C; +29.6 km for events more than 15 km from KEMF/NCHR. The median distance from 2 km bsf grew from 1.8 km (NLL3D) to 25.0 km (B4) for A-D. NLL3D was also wrong, by about 1.7 km too shallow, but its error is a shallow pin near the true band. Among the 26 near-station events, B4 raised the share in the 1.5–3.5 km band from 8 % to 12 % and the share below the Moho from 8 % to 31 %.

Recommended action

  1. Do not report or interpret Endeavour depths from B4 or NLL3D, including the 2 tier-B and 39 tier-C events. Add catalog flags for sig_z > 3 km, for maximum-likelihood depths at the grid top, and for depths below the Moho at a ridge.
  2. For the catalog, fix depth by the Endeavour convention (2 km bsf within 2 km of the vent fields, 3 km elsewhere; Krauss et al. 2023, Weekly et al. 2013) and relocate epicentres with depth fixed. Or use a depth prior of about 0–8 km bsf.
  3. Before the next B4 iteration, reset v0.3.0 to the starting model wherever resolution is zero. The pattern here suggests the D-B046 merge keeps reference-model changes outside resolved areas. Then rebuild the time grids and compare the KEMF, NCHR and J55C S station terms.
  4. Optional: check whether the Krauss and Wilcock (2024) ONC archive (doi:10.26022/IEDA/330498) holds 2011–2014 events near KEMF for a one-to-one match.

4. Explorer too deep depth unconstrained confidence: high

Seaward of the deformation front, B4 puts events at a median 38 km bsl, about 25 km below the oceanic Moho. The literature caps seismicity there at about 22 km bsl. The landward 2014 Nootka sequence is roughly consistent.

What the literature says

  • Hutchinson et al. (2019), SeaJade I (33 OBS, July–September 2010), bsl: 15–20 km on the primary Nootka fault zone (NFZ) faults and 6–15 km on the conjugate faults; event groups span 6–22 km. The Moho is at 12–14 km bsl seaward of the front. Faulting stops at about 20 km bsl on the primary faults.
  • Hutchinson et al. (2023), SeaJade II, bsl: every event group seaward of the front lies at 5–22 km. Landward, the Nootka sequence lies at 9–17 km (overriding plate) and 20–36 km (subducting plate). Moho 13–15 km bsl.
  • Hutchinson et al. (2020), 2014 Mw 6.4 Nootka sequence, bsl by implication: SE segment 5–15 km (overriding plate) and 19–26 km (subducting plate); NW segment mostly 20–36 km.
  • Braunmiller and Nábělek (2002), below seafloor: regional moment-tensor centroids, about three quarters at 4–10 km and about 14 offshore events at 12–15 km; seismogenic width about 10 km.
  • Hyndman and Rogers (1981): 12 OBS-located events at 2–6 km below the top of the crust. Cassidy and Rogers (1995): the main subevent of the 1992 Ms 6.8 Revere-Dellwood-Wilson earthquake at 4.3 km bsf.
  • Crust: 8–10 km (Malecek and Clowes 1978) and 6.4–11 km (Au and Clowes 1982) below seafloor.
  • Schaeffer et al. (2026), conference abstract, datum not stated: PACSAFE OBS relocations from near the seafloor to about 20 km, most at 5–10 km. Merrill et al. (2022), datum not stated: the 2014 mainshock deeper than 35 km.

Verdict and reasoning

Too deep seaward of the front. The B4 seaward median (37.8 km bsl) is about 16 km below the deepest literature bound (22 km bsl) and 20–25 km below the central values (about 12–16 km bsl, 9–10 km bsf). The Moho is at 12–15 km bsl, the seismogenic uppermost mantle is 5–7 km thick, and temperatures pass 500 °C by about 12 km bsl. Together these cap oceanic seismicity at about 20–22 km bsl. B4 puts the median event about 25 km below the Moho in lithosphere 0–5 Myr old. Even the best-placed subset is 5–10 km too deep. For the Nootka sequence, the NW part is consistent with Hutchinson et al. (2020) and the SE part is 2–4 km too deep at the median.

The dependence on station distance points to geometry-driven bias, not structure. Residuals are small (median P about 0 s, S +0.03 to +0.15 s), so the data do not resolve depth. One hypothesis: the model has no low-Vs sediment under the OBS, with water directly on 4.7 km/s crust. For tier-B event 77042, J69C at 10.9 km epicentral distance records S−P = 3.74 s. Even at 28.5 km bsl with a +0.25 s S−P station term, the S residual is +0.33 s. Unmodelled sediment S delays of 1–2 s would map into roughly 10–20 km of extra depth. This is a hypothesis to test, not a result.

Why B4 went deeper than NLL3D: under the seaward events (48.5–49.5°N, 130–128°W) v0.3.0 is about 0.5 km/s faster at 8 km bsl and about 0.1 km/s slower at 10–25 km, and mantle Vp/Vs at 10–30 km dropped from 1.775 to 1.73–1.75. Lower Vp/Vs means less S−P time per km, so the same S−P needs a longer path. For a source about 35 km deep that is roughly +1 km, the same order as the observed +2.3 km.

Effect of the tomography

Away from the literature. Paired B4 − NLL3D is +1.5 km for the region (A-D) and +2.3 km for seaward events (A-D, n = 647; 69 % deeper), +2.5 km for seaward A-C. The seaward p10 went from 9.5 to 21.1 km bsl. The share of seaward events in the 5–22 km bsl range halved, from 12 % to 6 % (A-D) and from 11 % to 4 % (A-C). The 107 seaward events that NLL3D placed above 15 km now have a B4 median of 25.9 km with sig_z 16.3 km. For the landward Nootka events the change is only +0.3 km. v4, whose datum is uncertain, was closer: median 9–12 km, with 48 % of events in 5–22 km bsl, although 22 % would plot above the seafloor.

See whether depths come back above about 20 km bsl.

  4. For ground truth, request the SeaJade II relocations (1,557 events, 17 April–30 May 2014) and match them with our 167 located events in that window. Also try SeaJade I (2010) and the Merrill et al. (2022) catalog.

Where depths look too shallow

No region has a B4 median more than about 1 km shallower than the literature. The shallow errors are pins at the top of the grid. NLL3D places 27–47 % of ridge events within 0.25 km of the seafloor at Endeavour and on the JdF ridge, and its northern Gorda Ridge median was 0.5 km bsf. B4 still pins 31 % of JdF events and about 25 % of northern Gorda Ridge events there. These are not depth measurements. Two smaller cases: B4 is 2.9 km shallower than Morton et al. (2023) on the Mendocino coast, but deeper than NCSN on the same events; and it is 0.8 km shallower than Lange et al. in the Cascadia Depression. Surface sources go the other way: quarry blasts near Texada Island sit at about 6 km and Puget explosions at about 3 km.

Where depths agree

Puget/Georgia (+0.3 km), the forearc benchmarks at Fortuna and Newport (0 ± 1.5 km), Oregon and SW Washington (within 0.2 km of PNSN and inside the published ranges), Mount St Helens (+0.5 to +0.8 km), the Cleft segment and the northern Gorda Ridge. In all of these, depth is controlled by close stations. The pattern across the catalog is that B4 agrees with the literature within about 1 km where stations are close and the S model is reasonable, and the error grows with distance to the nearest station and with weak S constraint. Axial is the exception: the stations are close but the model is non-physical.

Likely causes

  1. Velocity model v0.3.0. This is the main cause where the tomography moved events away from the literature.
    • Vp/Vs below about 1.6 at nodes with weak S coverage: 1.35–1.40 at Axial, 1.28–1.56 at Endeavour. 57 nodes in the upper 10 km have Vp/Vs below 1.5, at Axial, Endeavour, Gorda/Mendocino (41.2–41.3°N) and near Blanco (44.2–44.4°N). Low Vp/Vs reduces the S−P time per km, so a given S−P needs a longer path, and events under or near a station move down.
    • Moderately low Vp/Vs elsewhere: 1.66–1.71 at 12–29 km under Gorda; 1.63–1.68 at 9–15 km under Ferndale, where the literature finds high Vp/Vs; 1.73–1.75 in the Explorer mantle; 1.7–1.9 at Blanco, where the fault zone has more than 2.
    • Perturbations at nodes with zero resolution: Endeavour (dVp −15 to +19 %), Blanco above 15 km (±10–21 %). This points to the D-B046 merge keeping changes outside the resolved volume.
    • Weak smoothing in the offshore tiles: s60 for ridges_north and s20 for Blanco, against s480 onshore.
    • Hypothesis for Explorer: no low-velocity sediment under the OBS.
  2. Station geometry. Offshore, depth increases with distance to the nearest station: Gorda interior from 16 to 26 km bsf, Explorer from 25 to 45 km bsl. Outside OBS deployments (Blanco in 2014–2015, south of Endeavour, most of Explorer) depth is not constrained at all.
  3. Depth–origin-time trade-off. With 4–8 phases and the nearest station 30 km or more away, a seafloor solution and a 30–35 km solution both fit (JdF: RMS 0.116 s against 0.148 s). Tier labels and sig_z do not catch this: 21 JdF events are tier B at 31–36 km bsf, because the z* safeguard fails when v4 is also deep.
  4. Station terms. New S terms grew at Endeavour (KEMF +0.21 s, NCHR +0.34 s, J55C +0.32 s). They have not yet been checked at Axial or on the Mendocino coast.
  5. Datum. Not a cause of the main offsets. surface_km is elevation, positive up: the catalogs’ above_surface flag equals depth < −surface_km for 99.98 % (B4) and 99.99 % (NLL3D) of located events. Onshore and on the coast the surface is within 0.5 km of sea level, so the Mendocino offset cannot be a datum effect. Offshore studies mostly report depth below the seafloor, and the conversion is good to about 0.1 km at Axial. Open datum questions: Lange et al. (2026), which decides the Blanco West and Surveyor verdicts; most land-network studies, which do not state one; and v4, whose offset from NLL3D is larger than the water depth offshore.
  6. Picks, events and grids. Misidentified picks at BB200 (Blanco, near 127.9°W). Quarry blasts (1,605 at Texada Island, more in Puget and the Fraser Valley) in shallow statistics. Grid-node pile-ups: 10 % of NLL3D Axial events at 3.006 km bsl; the B4 Mendocino lower layer at the 29 km node.

Inputs

  • B4: /wd1/mdenolle_data/reloc_v5/catalog/catalog_b4_b4_iter1_v0.3.0.csv.
  • v4-NLL3D: /wd1/mdenolle_data/reloc_v5/catalog/catalog_v5_trackA_iter1_vpvs176.csv.
  • v4: /wd1/mdenolle_data/catalog_v4_0_backup/Cascadia_relocated_catalog_v4.csv.
  • ANSS (ComCat) 2010–2016: /home/mdenolle/cascadia_obs_ensemble/data/magnitude_v4/anss_2010-16.csv; 173,581 rows (nc 132,281, nn 23,842, uw 16,707, us 750).
  • All three of our catalogs have 172,340 rows in the same ID order (checked row by row). The v4_depth column in B4 and NLL3D equals depth(km) in the original v4 file to within 0.5 m. The region column is identical for every ID in B4 and NLL3D, so each event has a fixed region.

Depth column

  • Depth is the depth column: the NonLinLoc maximum-likelihood hypocentre, in km bsl, positive down. exp_depth (the PDF expectation) was not used; for B4 it runs about 0.4–0.5 km deeper (Axial median 5.43 against 4.96 km).
  • B4’s depth_frame column reads “true depth (EFT undone, b4_catalog.py)” for every nll3d row.
  • Depths are quantized on grid steps of about 0.07–0.15 km.

Event sets and percentiles

  • Located = tier A, B, C or D in each catalog’s own tier column (B4 157,270; NLL3D 157,267). A-C = tiers A, B, C. Tier U is excluded: those rows carry the v4 location and have surface_km = NaN.
  • Percentiles are p10/p50/p90 from numpy.percentile (linear interpolation) over finite values.

Sign of surface_km

  • surface_km is elevation, positive up. Region medians: Axial −1.55, Blanco −3.10, Gorda −3.07, Endeavour −2.58, Cascades +1.04 km.
  • Check: the catalogs’ above_surface flag equals depth < −surface_km for 99.98 % (B4) and 99.99 % (NLL3D) of located events. The opposite reading, depth < surface_km, matches only 95.7–96.0 %.
  • Depth below the local ground or seafloor is therefore depth + surface_km; negative values mean the event sits above the surface. Each catalog’s surface_km is sampled at its own epicentre; B4 and NLL3D differ by a median 0.03 km. The surface median quoted per region is over B4 located events.

v4 depths (datum unknown)

  • v4_depth is reported as given for all region events including tier U, for the B4 A-D events, and for the B4 A-C events. “Read as bsl, X % above surface” is the share of B4 A-D events with v4_depth + surface_km < 0.
  • Datum test: v4 minus NLL3D depth for NLL3D tier A-B events (about 54,000), against surface_km. A least-squares fit gives intercept −2.35 km and slope +0.95, but the binned medians are not linear. For surface between −0.2 and +1.5 km the offset is flat at −1.2 to −1.5 km; for +1.5 to +3 km it is −2.3 km. Offshore it is −2.8 km at −0.6, −3.7 km at −1.75 and −6.2 km where the surface is below −2.5 km.
  • v4 is therefore shallower than NLL3D by more than the water depth offshore. Neither a sea-level nor a local-surface datum reconciles v4 with the 3-D locations. We treat v4 depths as datum-ambiguous and do not judge them against the literature.

Paired change

B4 depth minus NLL3D depth for IDs located (A-D) in both catalogs, and separately for IDs tier A-C in both.

ANSS matching

  • ANSS time is parsed as UTC. For our catalogs we used the relocated origin time (time, UTC without “Z”), not v4_time.
  • Each catalog is matched separately, using its located (A-D) events only. Candidate pairs: |Δt| ≤ 3 s and haversine epicentral distance (R = 6371 km) ≤ 20 km between our relocated epicentre and the ANSS epicentre.
  • Assignment is one-to-one. Pairs are sorted by |Δt|, then by distance; each of our events keeps its best pair, then each ANSS event keeps its best remaining partner.
  • Matches: 15,635 for B4 and 15,648 for NLL3D. 15,588 IDs matched the same ANSS event in both catalogs; this common set is used. Median |Δt| is 0.3–0.4 s and median distance 1.9–2.4 km.
  • Further filters: ANSS type = earthquake, and the event is located (A-D) in both catalogs. Region comes from our catalog.
  • ANSS depth is ComCat depth as given, not converted. ComCat’s datum varies by network (sea level or geoid, or average station elevation). “Possibly fixed” flags ANSS rows with depthError = 0, or with depth an exact integer above 0 (for example us 10.0 km).
  • We report median ANSS, B4, NLL3D and v4 depths over the same matched events, plus median paired differences (B4 − ANSS, NLL3D − ANSS), and again for events tier A-C in both catalogs.
  • Matched events are biased to larger magnitudes (Gorda: matched median v4 magnitude 2.8 against 2.0 for all located events), so matched medians are not regional medians.
  • Global ANSS − NLL3D check on tier A-B events (n = 9,446): median by network uw −0.2 km, nc −2.9 km, us −5.9 km (us offshore depths are mostly fixed at 10 km). Where the surface is between −0.2 and +3 km, ANSS − NLL3D shows no consistent trend with elevation (−0.3, −0.5, −1.4, +0.0 km by bin), so no simple datum offset appears.

Region-specific analyses

  • Sub-areas, segments and boxes are defined in each region section (latitude and longitude limits, or nearest-station and sig_z bins).
  • Event-level matches with published catalogs: Lange et al. (2026) data release for Blanco (11,298 of 13,503 absolute events; median Δt 0.16 s, distance 1.7 km); Hirao et al. (2025) data release for Mount St Helens (|Δt| ≤ 2 s, ≤ 5 km, one-to-one greedy; 2,369 events); Morton et al. (2023) data release for the forearc (1,616 A-C matches). Statistics quoted for Guo et al. (2025) and Yoon and Shelly (2024) were recomputed from their data releases.
  • Model diagnostics (Vp, Vs, Vp/Vs, dws, resolution at nodes; S−P per km in the NonLinLoc grids) were read from the v0.3.0 model files and the B4 time grids.
  • All region scripts are in the scratch folders named in each section. Nothing in the repository or in the catalogs was changed.

How the literature was found and verified

  • For each region, candidate studies were collected by searching the web and bibliographic databases (Crossref, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar) and by following reference lists. The aim was studies that report earthquake depths, or depths of structures that bound seismicity (magma chambers, Moho, slab top, thermal limits), for the same area.
  • Each study was then checked in a separate pass. The DOI was resolved and the Crossref record compared with the citation (title, authors, journal, volume, pages, year). The text was read where we could reach it: the published paper, an accepted manuscript, an author copy, a PMC or NSF-PAR copy, or a dissertation chapter that reprints the paper. Every number we use was searched for in that text, and corrections were recorded (for example a range that turned out to be a magma-chamber depth, not a seismicity limit).
  • The datum of each study was recorded as stated in the text, read from figure axes (Wilcock et al. 2016, Waldhauser et al. 2020, Levy et al. 2018), inferred (for example a velocity model with no water layer), or “not stated”.
  • A study is listed as verified when the depth numbers we use were seen in its text or abstract; each reference shows which. It is listed as not verified when the main depth numbers could not be seen in the paper itself (paywall, blocked download, or the search budget ran out), even if the DOI and abstract were confirmed.
  • Two sources are conference abstracts and are not peer reviewed (Ren et al. 2025; Schaeffer et al. 2026). Lange et al. (2026) is published, but its depth sentences were checked in the arXiv preprint and its statistics come from the data release.
  • The searches, checks and comparisons were done by AI research agents (Claude) under the PI’s direction. No person has yet re-read the papers for this report. Numbers that go into the manuscript should be spot-checked against the papers.
