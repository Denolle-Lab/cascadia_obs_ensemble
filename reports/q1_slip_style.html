Q1: what sets how a plate boundary releases slip?

Swarms or mainshock-aftershock sequences, across the offshore Cascadia plate system. Catalog v6.1, D-B113 segments, D-B114 external data. Decision D-B115. Report built Fri Nov 27 14:01:34 PST 2026. Pre-registration written Fri Nov 27 13:26:14 PST 2026 and committed as 109915b8 (branch science/q1-slip-style) before any covariate correlation was computed.

Thermal control (T1, T2, T4): inconclusive. Plate age: cell rho -0.43 (n = 45, one-sided p 0.0016, Holm 0.0080, block 95 % interval -0.78 to +0.29); within boundary types +0.03 (Holm 1.0000). Heat flow: rho +0.30 (n = 29, Holm 0.2372, block -0.16 to +0.72); within types +0.11 (Holm 1.0000).

1. Question and hypothesis

Question. What sets how a plate boundary releases slip: in swarms, or in mainshock-aftershock sequences?

Working hypothesis (H1). Release is swarm-dominated where the lithosphere is young, hot, magmatic or fluid-rich (ridges, the Blanco pull-aparts, Mount St Helens) and mainshock-aftershock-dominated where it is colder and stronger (Gorda plate, Mendocino fault zone, Gorda slab). In testable form: the share of earthquakes in swarms rises with thermal state (younger plate, higher heat flow, shallower isotherms) and with fluid content (higher in-situ Vp/Vs), and it does so across spatial cells drawn without the segment boundaries, not only as a contrast between boundary types.

What was known before the registration, and so cannot confirm H1: the v6.1 plate-boundary table gave swarm shares of 29 % at Axial, 28 % at Mount St Helens, 25 % at Blanco and 19 % on the Gorda Ridge, against 2.8 % or less at the Mendocino fault zone, Gorda plate, triple junction and Gorda slab. That contrast is where H1 came from. It is shown below for completeness, but it carries no test.

4. Results

4.1 Segments

Swarm share above Mc separates two groups of segments. The ridges, the Blanco transform, Mount St Helens and the other Cascade arc events put 15-29 % of their earthquakes in swarms; the Gorda plate, Mendocino fault zone, triple junction, Gorda slab, the Juan de Fuca slab and the forearc put 0.7-4.7 %. The lowest segment of the hot group (Juan de Fuca ridge, 17.2 %) is six times the highest of the cold group (Gorda slab, 2.8 %). This contrast generated the hypothesis, so it is described here, not tested. b ranges from 0.74 (Juan de Fuca intraslab) and 0.80 (Axial) to 1.41 (Gorda Ridge) and 1.45 (Puget); it does not split along the hot and cold groups (Axial is among the lowest b, the Gorda Ridge among the highest). Productivity (direct offspring of events 1-2 units above Mc) is highest at Blanco (1.5) and Puget (1.4) and lowest at the Mendocino fault zone (0.25) and the Juan de Fuca slab (0.13): in swarm settings the chain-like families give each event offspring, so this measure does not isolate aftershock productivity.

4.2 Spatial cells and the primary tests

104 cells of 0.2 degree hold at least 50 events. Plate age exists in 45 of them (oceanic crust in the age grid: ridges, Blanco, the Mendocino fault zone, the Gorda plate and two triple-junction cells), heat flow in 29 (mostly onshore and around the triple junction; the smoothed field is empty at Blanco and at Axial).

T1, plate age. rho = -0.43 over 45 cells, one-sided p = 0.0016, Holm-adjusted 0.0080: significant with the predicted sign. The 1 x 1 degree block bootstrap gives a 95 % interval of -0.78 to +0.29, which includes 0. The 45 cells fall in 14 blocks, and the signal comes from one contrast. Of the 13 cells older than 2 Myr, one is a western Blanco cell (2.9 Myr, swarm share 59 %); the other 12 (southern Gorda plate, Mendocino fault zone, triple junction) have no swarm events at all. The cells younger than 2 Myr (ridges and Blanco) have a median swarm share of 10.5 %.

T4a, plate age within boundary types. Weighted within-type rho = +0.03, one-sided p = 0.5645 (Holm 1.0000). Per type: intraplate constant (5 cells); ridge +0.36 (7 cells); transform -0.05 (31 cells). The 31 transform cells are 25 Blanco and 6 Mendocino fault zone cells. Looked at after the test (not registered): the 25 Blanco cells alone (0.2-2.9 Myr, swarm share 0-59 %) give rho +0.51, the opposite sign to the prediction. The quasi-binomial model with type terms gives -1.25 +- 0.84 on the logit scale per standard deviation of age (dispersion 44).

T2 and T4b, heat flow. rho = +0.30 over 29 cells, one-sided p = 0.0593, Holm 0.2372; block interval -0.16 to +0.72. Within types +0.11 (Holm 1.0000; arc +0.41 (6 cells); other -0.35 (7 cells); subduction +0.40 (5 cells)); the type-controlled model does not converge (the subduction and crustal cells have no swarms, a separation), so it gives no effect size. With the interim heat-flow sampler (median of GHFDB sites within 25 km) T2 is -0.01: the heat-flow result depends on how sparse site values are spread to the events (with the interim values the heat-flow sign holds in 10 of 22 control runs, against 21 with the D-B114 field).

4.4 In-situ Vp/Vs of earthquake families

Of the 2,097 v5.1 families, 1,785 carry over to one v6.1 family (that family holds >= 50 % of their members). With the registered rules (CC-accepted P and S at >= 3 common stations per pair, pairs within 5 km, >= 5 events and >= 30 points per family), 245 families give an estimate and 27 are valid (95 % half-width <= 0.10, value within 1.4-2.5): 7 swarm, 18 mainshock-aftershock and 2 multiplet families. T3 is therefore not testable. None of the valid families is on a ridge; 8 are in the Mendocino block.

Two measurement limits explain the low yield. First, regression dilution: within a family the demeaned P differential times spread over only about 10 ms (median interquartile range), close to the CC noise (median residual MAD 5 ms). The L1 slope then falls toward zero as noise grows: the median estimate rises from 0.30 in the lowest fifth of signal-to-noise to 1.67 in the highest fifth, where the values (1.6-1.8) are physically plausible. Second, at Axial the S-P time is about 0.5 s, so the S window (-0.3 to +1.0 s around S) contains P energy; the 56 Axial estimates have a median of 0.90, below any rock value, with half-widths near 0.2. Catalog picks (the registered fallback) are worse: their noise exceeds the spread, the median estimate is 0.17 and 2 families are valid. The 11-25 feasibility count (621 families) counted catalog picks only and did not check precision.

For the record, not as a test: the valid swarm families have a median Vp/Vs of 1.65 (n = 7) and the mainshock-aftershock families 1.73 (n = 18), the opposite order to the hypothesis, on too few families to mean anything.

6. What the paper can claim, and what it cannot

Pre-registered outcome. Thermal control: inconclusive (T1 is significant with the predicted sign, the null rule passes and the sign holds in all 22 control runs, but the block-bootstrap interval of T1 includes 0, so D1 fails; T4 shows no age effect within boundary types). Not falsified: T1 and T2 both have the predicted sign, the hot group stays above the cold group at a uniform Mc, and the null rule passes. Fluid signal: not testable.

The paper can claim

  • Slip style splits by boundary setting: 17-29 % of earthquakes above Mc are in swarms on the ridges, the Blanco transform and at Mount St Helens, against 0.7-2.8 % on the Gorda plate, the Mendocino fault zone, the triple junction and the Gorda slab. The split survives a uniform Mc of 2.15 (reclustered: hot median 23 %, cold median 1.6 %), all 12 clustering variants, catalog v6.0, and a time-shuffle null.
  • In the cold settings, families carry a dominant mainshock far more often than random magnitude ordering would give (swarm share below the 5th percentile of a magnitude-shuffle null), while at Axial, Mount St Helens and the Juan de Fuca ridge the families are indistinguishable from random magnitude ordering.
  • Across 45 oceanic 0.2 degree cells, the swarm share falls with plate age (Spearman -0.43, Holm-adjusted p 0.008); of the cells older than 2 Myr only one western Blanco cell has swarms. Stated as a correlation that tracks the boundary settings, with its block interval.

The paper cannot claim

  • That plate age or temperature sets the slip style beyond the boundary type: within types the age effect is nil (weighted rho +0.03), and the cell correlation rests on 14 one-degree blocks (block interval -0.78 to +0.29). Young lithosphere and the swarm-prone settings coincide here, so these data cannot separate the two.
  • Any heat-flow control: T2 is not significant, measured heat flow is missing at Blanco and Axial, and the sign depends on how site values are spread.
  • A fluid (Vp/Vs) control on swarms: T3 is not testable (7 swarm and 18 mainshock-aftershock families with a valid Vp/Vs).
  • That sediment thickness or roughness control the style: those correlations are exploratory and follow the same geography as age.
  • Anything about individual families other than large sequences (D-B111).

8. Limitations

  • Families shift between catalog versions (D-B111): the shares here are over many families and change little between v6.0 and v6.1 (rank 0.96), but Mount St Helens moves from 38 % to 28 % because one family was retyped.
  • Mc differs between regions (0.65 at Axial to 2.15 on the Gorda Ridge and plate in the clustering; 0.35 at Axial and Mount St Helens in the segment Mc). The event set is above the regional Mc, and the uniform-Mc runs keep the contrast, but at Mc 2.15 Mount St Helens and several small segments drop below 100 events.
  • The swarm and mainshock-aftershock types come from topology and magnitude rules (Zaliapin and Ben-Zion) applied to families of >= 5 events; families of 2-4 events are multiplets or mainshock-aftershock pairs by the magnitude gap only. The magnitude-shuffle null shows that random magnitudes alone make many families swarms.
  • Plate age exists only on oceanic crust in the age grid, so the cell tests cover ridges, Blanco, the Mendocino fault zone, the Gorda plate and two triple-junction cells; the slab, the forearc and onshore settings enter only the segment contrast.
  • Spatial dependence: 45 age cells in 14 one-degree blocks. Neighbouring cells share families and structure, so the cell count overstates the independent samples; the block interval is the honest uncertainty.
  • Heat flow: GHFDB sites are sparse offshore, absent on the Blanco transform, and ridge-crest values are lowered by hydrothermal circulation; the smoothed field and the 25 km median give different results.
  • Spreading rate is the rate at which the crust formed (Seton et al. 2020 grid), not present-day plate motion; it varies little here (60-69 mm/yr in the plate-age cells).
  • Vp/Vs: the CC differential times within families spread over about 10 ms against about 5 ms of noise, so the slope is biased low except in the best-sampled families; at Axial the S window contains P energy (S-P about 0.5 s). A useful family Vp/Vs needs a P-free S window and families with a wider spread of station geometry.
  • No waveform double-difference catalog: swarm migration and duration are provisional.
  • Deep events under Mount St Helens are not interpreted (D-B111). The Mount St Helens event set holds 243 events within 25 km of the summit at any depth; 5 are deeper than 30 km and none of them is in a swarm. Without the 13 events deeper than 20 km the swarm share is 29.1 % (28.4 % with them).
