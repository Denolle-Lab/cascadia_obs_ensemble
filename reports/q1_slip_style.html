1. Question and hypothesis

Question. What sets how a plate boundary releases slip: in swarms, or in mainshock-aftershock sequences?

Working hypothesis (H1). Release is swarm-dominated where the lithosphere is young, hot, magmatic or fluid-rich (ridges, the Blanco pull-aparts, Mount St Helens) and mainshock-aftershock-dominated where it is colder and stronger (Gorda plate, Mendocino fault zone, Gorda slab). In testable form: the share of earthquakes in swarms rises with thermal state (younger plate, higher heat flow, shallower isotherms) and with fluid content (higher in-situ Vp/Vs), and it does so across spatial cells drawn without the segment boundaries, not only as a contrast between boundary types.

What was known before the registration (as written in it), and so cannot confirm H1: the v6.1 plate-boundary table of 10:07 PST (PB2002 segments) gave swarm shares of 29 % at Axial, 28 % at Mount St Helens, 25 % at Blanco and 19 % on the Gorda Ridge, against 2.8 % or less at the Mendocino fault zone, Gorda plate, triple junction and Gorda slab. That contrast is where H1 came from. It is shown below for completeness, but it carries no test.

4. Results

4.1 Segments

Swarm share above Mc separates two groups of segments. The hot group (Axial, the Juan de Fuca ridge, the Gorda Ridge, Blanco and Mount St Helens) puts 18.3-29.2 % of its earthquakes in swarms; the cold group (the Gorda plate, the Mendocino fault zone, the triple junction and the Gorda slab) 0.7-2.8 %. The lowest hot segment (Juan de Fuca ridge, 18.3 %) is 6.5 times the highest cold segment (Gorda slab, 2.8 %). The other segments range from 0.0 % (Juan de Fuca plate) to 15.4 % (Cascade arc). This contrast generated the hypothesis, so it is described here, not tested. b ranges from 0.74 (Juan de Fuca slab) and 0.80 (Axial) to 1.41 (Gorda Ridge) and 1.45 (Puget); it does not split along the hot and cold groups (Axial 0.80, Gorda Ridge 1.41). 4.2 Spatial cells and the primary tests

104 cells of 0.2 degree hold at least 50 events. Plate age exists in 45 of them (oceanic crust in the age grid: ridges, Blanco, the Mendocino fault zone, the Gorda plate and 2 triple-junction cells), heat flow in 29 (mostly onshore and around the triple junction; the smoothed field has no value at Blanco and Axial).

T1, plate age. rho = -0.43 over 45 cells, one-sided p = 0.0016, Holm-adjusted 0.0080: significant with the predicted sign. The 1 x 1 degree block bootstrap gives a 95 % interval of -0.78 to +0.29, which includes 0. The 45 cells fall in 14 blocks, and the signal comes from one contrast. Of the 13 cells older than 2 Myr, one has swarm events (a Blanco cell at 2.9 Myr, swarm share 59 %) and the other 12 (the Gorda plate, the Mendocino fault zone and the triple junction) have none. 4.4 In-situ Vp/Vs of earthquake families

Of the 2,097 v5.1 families, 1,785 carry over to one v6.1 family (that family holds >= 50 % of their members). With the registered rules (CC-accepted P and S at >= 3 common stations per pair, pairs within 5 km, >= 5 events and >= 30 points per family), 245 families give an estimate and 27 are valid (95 % half-width <= 0.10, value within 1.4-2.5): 7 swarm, 18 mainshock-aftershock and 2 multiplet families. T3 is therefore not testable. None of the valid families is on a ridge; the most are in the mendocino CC block (8).

Two measurement limits explain the low yield. First, regression dilution: within a family the demeaned P differential times spread over only about 10 ms (median interquartile range), close to the CC noise (median residual MAD 5 ms). The L1 slope then falls toward zero as noise grows: the median estimate rises from 0.30 in the lowest fifth of signal-to-noise to 1.67 in the highest fifth, where the valid values span 1.41-1.84. Second, at Axial the S-P time is about 0.5 s, so the S window (-0.3 to +1.0 s around S) contains P energy; the 56 Axial estimates have a median of 0.90, below any rock value, with a median half-width of 0.24. Catalog picks (the registered fallback) are worse: their noise exceeds the spread, the median estimate is 0.17 and 2 families are valid. The 11-25 feasibility count (621 families) counted catalog picks only and did not check precision.

For the record, not as a test: the valid swarm families have a median Vp/Vs of 1.65 (n = 7) and the mainshock-aftershock families 1.73 (n = 18), the opposite order to the hypothesis, on too few families to mean anything.

Shuffled-catalog nulls. Time shuffle (100 catalogs, event times permuted within each clustering region, real thresholds): Axial, Blanco, the Gorda Ridge and Mount St Helens lie above the 95th percentile of their null swarm share, so the null rule passes (4 of 4). Dense seismicity alone makes swarms at Axial (null mean 14.7 %, observed 29.2 %); elsewhere the null mean is 0.0-3.5 %. Magnitude shuffle (200 draws, family membership and trees kept): in the cold segments the observed swarm share is below the 5th percentile of the magnitude-shuffle null (Gorda plate 0.7 % against 57 %; Mendocino fault zone 1.1 % against 12 %; triple junction 1.0 % against 13 %; Gorda slab 2.8 % against 12 %), so their families carry a dominant mainshock far more often than random magnitudes would give. The Gorda Ridge and Blanco sit in between (below the 5th percentile, at 20 % and 25 %).

6. What the paper can claim, and what it cannot

Pre-registered outcome. Thermal control: inconclusive (T1 is significant with the predicted sign, the null rule passes, the sign holds in 22 of 22 control runs, but the block-bootstrap interval of T1 includes 0, so D1 fails; T4a gives +0.03 within boundary types). Falsification rules: none applies. Fluid signal: not testable.

The paper can claim

  • Slip style splits by boundary setting: 18-29 % of earthquakes above Mc are in swarms at Axial, the Juan de Fuca ridge, the Gorda Ridge, Blanco and Mount St Helens, against 0.7-2.8 % at the Gorda plate, the Mendocino fault zone, the triple junction and the Gorda slab. The split survives a uniform Mc of 2.15 (reclustered: hot median 23.5 %, cold median 1.5 %), the 12 clustering variants and catalog v6.0 (lowest hot above highest cold in 18 of 18 runs), and a time-shuffle null.
  • At the Gorda plate, the Mendocino fault zone, the triple junction and the Gorda slab the families carry a dominant mainshock far more often than random magnitude ordering would give (swarm share below the 5th percentile of a magnitude-shuffle null), while at Axial, the Juan de Fuca ridge and Mount St Helens the families cannot be told apart from random magnitude ordering.
  • Across 45 oceanic 0.2 degree cells, the swarm share falls with plate age (Spearman -0.43, Holm-adjusted p 0.0080); of the 13 cells older than 2 Myr, one has swarms. Stated as a correlation that tracks the boundary settings, with its block interval.
  • Over the 8 oceanic segments the swarm share falls with plate age (Spearman -0.93, BH 0.0154, a secondary test); it is significant only with the v2 boundaries (D-B113 segments: -0.79, BH 0.0847); report it with that dependence.

The paper cannot claim

  • That plate age or temperature sets the slip style beyond the boundary type: within types the age effect is +0.03 (weighted rho), and the cell correlation rests on 14 one-degree blocks (block interval -0.78 to +0.29). Young lithosphere and the swarm-prone settings coincide here, so these data cannot separate the two.
  • Any heat-flow control: T2 is not significant (rho +0.30, Holm 0.2372), measured heat flow is missing at Blanco and Axial, and the sign depends on how site values are spread.
  • A fluid (Vp/Vs) control on swarms: T3 is not testable (7 swarm and 18 mainshock-aftershock families with a valid Vp/Vs).
  • That sediment thickness or roughness control the style: those correlations are exploratory and follow the same geography as age.
  • Anything about individual families other than large sequences (D-B111).

8. Limitations

  • Families shift between catalog versions (D-B111): the shares here are over many families and change little between v6.0 and v6.1 (segment rank correlation 0.98), but Mount St Helens moves from 38 % to 28 % because one family was retyped.
  • Mc differs between regions (0.65 at Axial to 2.15 at Gorda in the clustering regions; segment Mc 0.35 at Axial and 0.35 at Mount St Helens). The event set is above the regional Mc, and the uniform-Mc runs keep the contrast, but at Mc 2.15 Mount St Helens drops below 100 events.
  • The swarm and mainshock-aftershock types come from topology and magnitude rules (Zaliapin and Ben-Zion) applied to families of >= 5 events; families of 2-4 events are multiplets or mainshock-aftershock pairs by the magnitude gap only. The magnitude-shuffle null shows that random magnitudes alone make many families swarms.
  • Plate age exists only on oceanic crust in the age grid, so the cell tests cover ridges, Blanco, the Mendocino fault zone, the Gorda plate and a few triple-junction cells; the slab, the forearc and onshore settings enter only the segment contrast.
  • Spatial dependence: 45 age cells in 14 one-degree blocks. Neighbouring cells share families and structure, so the cell count overstates the independent samples; the block interval is the honest uncertainty.
  • Heat flow: GHFDB sites are sparse offshore, absent on the Blanco transform, and ridge-crest values are lowered by hydrothermal circulation; the smoothed field and the 25 km median give different results.
  • Spreading rate is the rate at which the crust formed (Seton et al. 2020 grid), not present-day plate motion; it varies little here (60-69 mm/yr in the plate-age cells).
  • Vp/Vs: the CC differential times within families spread over about 10 ms against about 5 ms of noise, so the slope is biased low except in the best-sampled families; at Axial the S window contains P energy (S-P about 0.5 s). A useful family Vp/Vs needs a P-free S window and families with a wider spread of station geometry.
  • No waveform double-difference catalog: swarm migration and duration are provisional.
  • Deep events under Mount St Helens are not interpreted (D-B111). The Mount St Helens event set holds 243 events within 25 km of the summit at any depth; 5 are deeper than 30 km, none of them in swarms. Without the 13 events at 20 km or deeper the swarm share is 29.1 % (28.4 % with them).
  • Segment boundaries changed twice after the registration (D-B113, then plate boundaries v2, D-B117); the comparison in the section Boundaries v2 shows what moved.
