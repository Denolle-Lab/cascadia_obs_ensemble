Station terms for the v6.0 relocation: test on three new clusters

Track B, 2026-11-25. Branch reloc/v6-termtest, code workflow/10_tomography/b4_v6_termtest*.py. Data /wd1/mdenolle_data/reloc_v6/termtest/. A = static station terms (v5.1) + NLL-coherence; B = NLL-SSST stopped at 2 km + NLL-coherence.

Summary

Recommendation: B (NLL-SSST stopped at 2 km, then the coherence stage). The rule was written before the scores: B had to win the held-out-station test in at least 2 of 3 clusters and lose in none. It won in all 3, on P and on S, and no arm put an event above the surface.

What was tested

Three clusters that share no event with the four earlier sets: 1,000 events in the Puget Lowland (onshore, about 17 km deep), 600 events of the 2015 M5.7 sequence off Cape Mendocino (NC and PB land stations plus 7D and Z5 ocean-bottom seismometers) and 1,200 events of the October 2012 swarm on the western Blanco transform (X9 and 7D ocean-bottom seismometers). Every arm used the same picks (v4.1tc), the same v5.1 grids and the v6-prep code. CC differential times were measured for these clusters with the tested D-B078 code: 42,400 accepted rows in Puget, 22,100 at Mendocino and 38,700 at western Blanco.

Held-out stations (the leak-free test)

Each arm was rerun three times, each time without one third of the stations, and scored on the CC times at the stations it never saw (-18's scorer). Median misfit (MAD) on the same rows, A then B, with the 95 % interval of B - A:

  • Puget: P 85 vs 59 ms (B - A -26 ms, [-32, -20]); S 141 vs 90 ms (-51, [-58, -44]).
  • Mendocino: P 67 vs 56 ms (-12, [-15, -8]); S 118 vs 88 ms (-30, [-36, -24]).
  • western Blanco: P 149 vs 107 ms (-41, [-46, -37]); S 264 vs 164 ms (-100, [-107, -92]).

CC fit and depths (full runs)

The median per-pair residual of the CC times favours B in all three clusters: Puget 16.9 vs 14.8 ms, Mendocino 47.1 vs 41.9 ms, western Blanco 41.5 vs 38.6 ms (all intervals exclude 0). The repeaters (true separation about 0.1 km) are mixed: no difference in Puget (0.46 vs 0.53 km), B tighter at Mendocino (1.06 vs 0.38 km), A tighter at western Blanco (0.71 vs 1.77 km; the 2 km SSST pass spreads them there, they sit at 0.81-0.95 km after the 8 and 4 km passes). Depths: against Lange et al. (2026) at western Blanco, B is closer (median |dz| 2.11 vs 1.52 km, 891 events); against PNSN in Puget (345 events, A 1.35, B 1.22 km) and NCSN at Mendocino (39 events, both about 6 km deeper than NCSN, the known offset) there is no material difference. No event is above the surface in any arm.

Why the gap is large, and how this fits -18's result

The coherence stage of the v6-prep code relocates only events whose error ellipsoid is at most 5 km long. In the held-out runs that is 16-32 % of the located events in Puget, 42-56 % at Mendocino and 0.4-2 % at western Blanco. For every other event A keeps its static-term location and B its SSST location, so most of the difference is static terms against SSST. On the rows where the coherence stage relocated both events in both recipes, B is still better, but by less: Puget P -3.5 ms, S -4.5 ms; Mendocino P -4.5 ms, S -13 ms (intervals exclude 0). Part of B's gain comes from re-estimating station terms on the cluster itself: the first SSST pass (one term per station-phase, CharDist 9999 km) already beats the catalog-wide v5.1 terms (P 91 vs 101 ms in Puget, 69 vs 79 ms at Mendocino, 113 vs 149 ms at western Blanco).

This agrees with -18's four-set result in direction. -18's coherence on SSST stopped at 2 km beat coherence on the static terms by 0 to 9 ms (equal at Axial within 1 ms), without intervals; their coherence stage relocated every event, which is close to the "both relocated" rows here, where the differences are of the same size. It also agrees with -18 that fine SSST passes hurt: here the 2 km pass is already slightly worse than the 4 km pass in all three clusters (held-out P MAD after the 4 km and after the 2 km pass: Puget 64 and 68 ms, Mendocino 43 and 48 ms; at western Blanco the 16 km pass is best, 95 ms against 107 ms at 2 km). That comparison was made after seeing the scores, so it is a lead for a later check, not a result.

Two things the PI should know

  • At Mendocino, SSST alone does better than B on the held-out stations (P 48 vs 56 ms, S 68 vs 88 ms): there the coherence stage moves SSST locations away from what the unseen stations say. In Puget it helps (68 to 59 ms P). B still beats A everywhere, so the choice between A and B does not change.
  • The verdict is about the recipe as the v6-prep code runs it (coherence on compact events only, v5.1 grids, cluster-sized search boxes). The v6.0 run will compute SSST on the whole catalog; its first pass then gives catalog-wide terms again, and the local gain comes from the 16 to 2 km passes.
Recommendation (decision rule written before the scores): B. Held-out wins: A 0, B 3 of 3 clusters; events above the surface: A 0, B 0.

Follow-up: where to stop the SSST schedule (16, 8, 4 or 2 km)

Choice: stop the SSST schedule at 4 km (9999, 16, 8, 4 km), by the rule written in RESUME.md before any run of this test. With the coherence stage, the held-out score (MAD P + MAD S, mean of the two clusters) is 81.0 ms at 16 km, 78.4 at 8 km, 76.8 at 4 km and 80.2 at 2 km. The next coarser stop, 8 km, is worse than 4 km by 1.6 ms (95 % interval 0.3 to 2.7 ms), so the rule keeps 4 km. Stopping at 2 km costs 3.4 ms (1.8 to 4.6) against 4 km. The gaps are small, 2 to 5 % of the score, but they point the same way as the post-hoc hint from the first three clusters.

Clusters

Two new clusters that share no event with the seven earlier ones: 700 events in the Strait of Georgia (onshore, about 15 km deep, CN, PB, UW and TA stations) and 800 events of the 2014 M6.8 Gorda plate sequence (ZBZ family 1162, Z5 and 7D ocean-bottom seismometers with NC, PB, BK and TA land stations). CC data came from the same tested code: 42,000 accepted rows at Georgia and 23,400 at Gorda. Each run used one SSST chain, with oct-trees saved after the 16, 8, 4 and 2 km passes and the coherence stage run on each.

Held-out stations, per cluster

  • Georgia, with coherence, P / S MAD: 20.1 / 31.5 ms at 16 km, 20.2 / 30.8 at 8 km, 19.2 / 29.6 at 4 km, 20.4 / 31.1 at 2 km. The 4 km stop is lowest on both phases.
  • Gorda, with coherence: 48.1 / 62.2 ms at 16 km, 46.7 / 59.2 at 8 km, 47.1 / 57.7 at 4 km, 49.2 / 59.7 at 2 km. The curve is flat between 8 and 4 km, and 2 and 16 km are both worse.

Does the coherence stage help?

At Georgia it helps a lot, at every stop: P drops by about 15 ms (34.0 to 19.2 ms at 4 km) and S by about 21 ms, to values below those of the v5.1 catalog, which saw the held-out stations (26.5 / 43.4 ms). At Gorda it changes nothing that matters (-0.4 to +1.2 ms). There the stage relocates few events, because most Gorda events have error ellipsoids longer than 5 km: 0.5 to 22 % of the located events in the fold runs, against 41 to 53 % at Georgia. Over the five independent clusters tested today, coherence helped where it relocates many events (Puget, Georgia), hurt at Mendocino, and was neutral where it relocates few (Gorda, western Blanco).

Other criteria (full runs)

  • CC fit (median per-pair residual): Georgia lowest at 4 km with coherence (13.8 ms; 14.0 at 8 km, 14.3 at 2 km). At Gorda the 16 and 8 km stops fit best (18.8 and 18.6 ms), 4 km is at 20.1 ms and 2 km at 25.9 ms, so the 2 km stop is clearly worse.
  • Repeaters: Georgia 168 pairs, median separation 0.24 km at 4 km with coherence (0.26 to 0.29 km at the other stops, 0.58 km in v5.1). Gorda has only 15 pairs; its intervals overlap at every stop.
  • Depths: no stop is distinguishable from another. All arms sit about 2.7 km deeper than PNSN at Georgia (138 events; v5.1 2.4 km) and about 6 km deeper than NCSN at Gorda (27 events, the known Mendocino-area offset).

Method

Why this test

On 2026-11-25 at 14:29 the PI chose a test over a decision between two station-term recipes for the v6.0 NonLinLoc relocation. Recipe A is the v5.1 static station terms followed by the NLL-coherence stage. Recipe B is NLL-SSST with the CharDist schedule 9999, 16, 8, 4, 2 km (it stops at 2 km) followed by the same coherence stage. The two earlier tests (the v6-prep test and -18's D-B078 comparison) used the same four clusters (axial, blanco, msh, jdf), so this test uses three clusters that share no event with them.

Clusters

Clusters come from the v5.1 clustering (events_zbz_v51.csv.gz, families_zbz_v51.csv) and are cut like -18's sets: the located events nearest the median epicentre. Puget Lowland has no ZBZ family above 205 events, so the onshore set is spatial: the densest spot of v5.1 epicentres in the Puget/Georgia region (Kitsap Peninsula, about 17 km deep), 1,000 events. The Mendocino set is family 1536, the 2015-01-28 M5.7 offshore Cape Mendocino sequence, recorded by NC, BK, PB and TA land stations and by the 7D and Z5 ocean-bottom seismometers of Cascadia Initiative year 4 (600 events). The offshore set is family 427, the October 2012 swarm on the western Blanco transform, recorded by the Blanco OBS experiment (X9) and 7D (1,200 events).

Relocation

Every arm runs the v6-prep code (b4_v6_ssst.py) unchanged in what it computes: the clock-corrected picks v4.1tc (md5 checked), a2_make_obs.py, the v5.1 travel-time grids (the 1 km grid, or the 2 km wide grid for a station whose 1 km grid does not cover the search box), a search box of the set's epicentres plus 30 km and z' from -5 to 80 km, NLLoc with the v5.1 settings (LOCGAU 0.2 s, at least 5 phases, the merged-surface floor), Loc2ssst with the v6-prep LSPHSTAT and WeightFloor, and the coherence stage of b4_v6_ssst.coherence_run. That stage relocates only events whose 68 % ellipsoid is at most 5 km long (Len3), by stacking the oct-tree pdfs of the event and of its partners with pair coherence of at least 0.5; the other events keep their previous location. Pair coherence is the median cc_max of the pair's measured CC rows (at least 3 rows). The static arm is the static_oct run (static terms, oct-trees saved): in the v6-prep axial run its locations are identical to the run without oct-trees.

CC data

The CC differential times come from the tested D-B078 code (tomo/cc-dt 2d033ec6), with its parameters and the 3-15 Hz band that D-B093 uses for every block except Mount St Helens. Only the output folder and the set names change. Snippets that the D-B093 all-family job had already read were copied from its cache (read only); the others were read from pnwstore with the same reader. NC and BK snippets (Mendocino) come from the D-B093 NCEDC cache. Rows that straddle a confirmed D-B090 clock epoch (MH07.XD, MG07.XD) are flagged as in D-B078.

Criterion 1: held-out stations

This is -18's protocol and scorer (b5_cmp_common.fold_score from tomo/b5 b0dffcdd). The stations with picks in a set are dealt into 3 folds with a fixed seed. For each fold every arm is rerun from the beginning without the picks of that fold's stations, and the coherence stage uses no CC row at those stations, so no position or pair weight has seen the held-out stations. The held-out CC differential times are then predicted from each arm's positions in the v5.1 grids; each residual has the pair's median training residual removed, which cancels the origin-time offset. MAD and rms are pooled over the folds. The one change from -18's scorer: the prediction uses the grid NLLoc used for that station (1 km or 2 km wide grid) instead of always the 1 km grid, because in the western Blanco set 91 of 143 station-phases have no 1 km grid over the box and -18's lookup would be clipped at the grid edge. Arms are compared on the rows whose two events are located in every arm of the fold.

Criterion 2: fit of the CC times

On the full runs (all stations), the median per-pair residual of b4_v6_ssst_metrics.cc_score: for each accepted CC row, the observed minus the predicted differential arrival time with each arm's own corrections, minus the pair's median, then the median absolute value. This criterion is not independent of recipe B, whose SSST corrections are fitted to the same stations, and it does not test the coherence stage against data it has not seen. The repeater check takes the CC repeaters (median CC at least 0.95 over at least 6 station-phases), whose true separations are about 0.1 km, and reports the median 3-D distance each arm gives them.

Criterion 3: depths

Full runs only, against published catalogs: Lange et al. (2026) absolute OBS locations for western Blanco (the pair table of b4_lit_paired.py), PNSN (ANSS ComCat net uw) for Puget, NCSN (net nc) for Mendocino. ComCat events are paired one-to-one with the v5.1 events, greedy by origin-time difference then distance (Puget 2 s and 10 km, Mendocino 3 s and 20 km). The same events are used for every arm. Also counted: events above the merged solid surface (must be 0) and events at the NLLoc floor.

Statistics and decision rule

Intervals are 95 % percentile intervals from 2,000 paired bootstrap resamples (event pairs for criteria 1 and 2, with all of a pair's rows kept together; events for criterion 3). A difference is material when the interval of B - A excludes zero and the difference is at least 5 % of A (0.2 km for depths). The rule, written before any score of these runs: B is recommended only if it is materially better on held-out stations in at least 2 of the 3 clusters, materially worse in none, and puts no event above the surface; A on the mirror condition; otherwise "no material difference", and then A because it is simpler. Criteria 2 and 3 can only veto: a winner of criterion 1 that loses materially on both of them in 2 clusters is not recommended.
