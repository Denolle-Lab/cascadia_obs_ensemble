- **[high]** The pick-time offset between our picks and the model's training alignment is not quantified. FM_ML templates come from the 'Felix' .mat struct, which has a per-station DDt_{STA} 'P-arrival travel-time offset' field (FM_ML/01-scripts/convert_mat_to_h5.py lines 25 and 86). That suggests CC- or analyst-refined alignment, not ML picks. Training jitter is sigma 0.02 s clipped at ±0.08 s. The plan's shift ensemble covers only ±0.03 s (±3 samples) and requires shift_agree_frac = 1. Bias in PhaseNet/ensemble picks on emergent OBS onsets (often 0.05-0.15 s late, which is unmeasured here) would put the true onset outside the 64-sample window's centre, or before it.
  - Fix: In M1, measure the onset-minus-pick distribution per network and sensor class using first_motion_sign() on high-SNR picks, and on the PNSN/NCSN labelled picks against analyst times. If the median offset or the spread exceeds about 0.02 s, add a re-centring step (STA/LTA or AIC onset within ±0.2 s of the pick) before the 64-sample crop. Set the shift-ensemble range from the measured spread instead of a fixed ±3 samples. Report coverage lost to the shift_agree_frac = 1 rule.
- **[high]** The S/P noise question marked UNKNOWN is answered in the code, and it creates a mismatch with SKHASH. workflow/05_magnitude/route_a_day_amplitudes.py line 65 sets NOISE = (11.0, 1.0): noise is the RMS from tp-11 s to tp-1 s relative to each pick, taken as the maximum over components (README line 656). So noise for an S pick is P coda, not pre-event noise. It is also RMS, while amp_p/amp_s are peak values, and SKHASH's ratmin/qbadfrac assume like-for-like amplitudes. Signal amplitudes are also the maximum over all components, not Z for P and horizontal for S. And the amps sensor is often HN (NC 57k, CN 28k, TA 26k, XD 19k, BK 17k) while the polarity stage will choose velocity Z, so polarity and S/P can come from different sensors at the same pick.
  - Fix: In p06, take noise_p from the polarity window (peak absolute value over P-3 s to P-0.1 s on the same band). Pass noise_s = noise_p, or a peak measured on pre-P noise, rather than the RMS of P coda. Either convert RMS to peak (×√2 for Gaussian noise is only approximate) or remeasure. Record the S/P sensor and the polarity sensor separately. Flag pairs where they differ, and decide (D5) whether to redo P and S amplitudes on the velocity sensor's Z and horizontals.
- **[high]** Takeoff-angle uncertainty is not planned. The plan passes NLL ANGLES_YES grid values from the A2/A3 maximum-likelihood hypocentre, but says nothing about how takeoff_uncertainty is derived. Offshore events recorded by seafloor OBS have shallow source-receiver depth differences, so takeoffs sit near the horizontal or vertical. Small depth errors there move rays across nodal planes, and with a 1D vs 3D model difference the takeoff can change a lot. SKHASH's default 1D look_dep (0-39 km) also uses station elevation 0, while OBS sit at -1 to -3 km.
  - Fix: Compute per-pick takeoff and azimuth spread by querying the angle grids at 20-50 samples from each NLL location's PDF scatter, and pass that standard deviation as takeoff_uncertainty and azimuth_uncertainty (with a floor of about 5°). Make sure station depth below sea level is used (NLL grids use real elevations, so check that the SKHASH station file does not reset them to 0). Run a sensitivity test comparing 1D SKHASH tables and 3D NLL takeoffs on 200 A/B events.

- **[medium]** Hydrophones are dropped entirely, which leaves out a cheap OBS reversal check. For an up-going P at the seafloor, Z first motion and pressure first motion share a sign once the instrument's DPG/hydrophone sign convention is known. pnwstore has HDH for 7D, Z5 and OO, but the inventories lack ?DH metadata, and SIO and WHOI DPG conventions are opposite.
  - Fix: Keep HDH out of the polarity product (D4), but add it to §5 as a per-instrument-type QC. Fetch HDH StationXML from FDSN, group stations by OBS contractor (LDEO/WHOI/SIO), and check the Z vs HDH first-motion sign on high-SNR picks. A station whose Z disagrees with HDH while its contractor group agrees is a reversal candidate. This needs no new labels.
- **[medium]** OBS clock and timing problems are not mentioned. CI OBS (7D, X9, Z5) rely on skew corrections from end-of-deployment clock comparisons, applied linearly or flagged as uncorrected by the contractor. The leap seconds of 2012-06-30 and 2015-06-30 fall inside deployments. A constant clock error does not hurt a window centred on our own pick, but it biases locations and therefore takeoffs and S-P, and pick-vs-predicted residuals at the station level.
  - Fix: For each OBS deployment, record whether the DMC data are skew-corrected (from FDSN/DMC reports or the IRIS data-quality flags in miniSEED headers). Flag stations with uncorrected or large skew. Check that Track A station terms absorb constant offsets and that A2 residuals have no linear time trends per deployment. Carry an obs_clock_flag column into the polarity table.

The candidate actually named is the SeisPolarity SCSN re-train, whose inference does max-abs normalization only, with no filter. The training data behind 'tepicc' are undocumented, so the same PNW-dataset leakage that rules out SeisPolarity GLOBAL may apply.
  - Fix: In M0, read SeisPolarity's Ross model code and config: number of classes, input length, filter. Treat it as a separate candidate from Ross 2018. Confirm the SeisBench version and EquiPol/RPNet weight metadata from PyPI and the model card. List the datasets behind tepicc, and exclude it from PNSN validation if PNW is among them.

- **[medium]** The decimation-filter risk is aimed at the wrong place, and the bandpass causality default is not stated. axial_test_extract.py uses scipy decimate(ftype='fir', zero_phase=True). Its pre-ringing sits near the new Nyquist (above about 40 Hz) and is mostly removed by the 3-20 Hz band. The main causality risk is the 4th-order 3-20 Hz Butterworth in polarity_eval.bandpass, which defaults to zero_phase=True (sosfiltfilt) with the docstring 'assumed; UNKNOWN which FM_ML used'. Zero-phase IIR filtering causes precursory ringing that can flip emergent first motions. Separately, the 5% cosine taper applied to the full window is harmless only if the window is long; on an 8 s window it covers 0.4 s at each end, which is fine, but on short windows it would reach P.
  - Fix: Keep the decimation as is, and do the causal vs zero-phase A/B on the Butterworth bandpass. Score it on the Axial labels from Zhang and on model-free first_motion_sign agreement. Write the chosen filter into contract_hash.
